### AN ACT to provide for the referral of important questions of interpretation of the Constitution and the constitutionality of legislation enacted by Parliament.

Commencement [11 July 2005]

### Preliminary

### 1. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Attorney General's Reference (Constitutional Questions) Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

“**Constitution**” means the Constitution of Saint Lucia;

“**Court**” means the Court of Appeal.

### 3. Referring questions for opinion

(1) The Attorney General may, with the approval of Cabinet, refer to the Court for hearing and consideration, important questions of law (or fact) concerning—

(a) the interpretation of the Constitution;

(b) the constitutionality or interpretation of any legislation enacted by Parliament;

(c) any matter, whether or not in the opinion of the Court ejusdem generis with the matters contained in paragraphs (a) and (b) submitted by the Attorney General as an important question.

### 4. Questions deemed important

A question concerning any of the matters mentioned in section 3, and referred to the Court by the Attorney General, shall be deemed to be an important question.

### 5. Opinion of Court

Where a reference is made to the Court under section 3, the Court shall—

(a) hear and consider it;

(b) answer each question so referred;

(c) certify to the Attorney General its opinion on each question, with the reasons for each answer,

and the opinion shall be pronounced in like manner as in the case of a judgment on appeal to the Court

### 6. Notice to interested persons

The Court has power to direct that any person interested or, where there is a class of persons interested, any one or more persons as representatives of that class, shall be notified of the hearing on a reference under this Act and those persons are entitled to be heard thereon.

### 7. Appointment of Counsel by Court

The Court may, in its discretion, request any counsel to argue the case with respect to any interest that is affected and with respect to which counsel does not appear, and the reasonable expenses thereby occasioned may be paid by the Minister responsible for finance out of monies appropriated by Parliament for expenses of litigation.

### 8. Savings

The provisions of this Act do not derogate from the powers of the Court under any other enactment.
