### AN ACT to consolidate and amend the law with regard to public medical officers.

Commencement [26 June 1903]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Medical Officers Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act unless modified by the context—

“**medical officer**” means a Government medical officer whatever his or her official title or designation or duties for the time being may be;

“**pauper**” means a person who is not possessed of property or income who through prolonged illness, or chronic ailment or age or infirmity is unable to earn a living;

“**medical aid**” means medical attendance, medical advice, medical and surgical treatment and furnishing of medicine by the medical officer, or under his or her order by a Government dispenser;

“**serious illness**” means illness where immediate medical aid is imperatively required to avert serious consequences to the patient;

“**worker**” means any person whose income does not exceed $720 a year or who does not possess property of any kind worth more than $1200.

*(Amended by Act 3 of 1966)*

*Medical Districts and Officers*

### 3. Division of Saint Lucia into Districts

The Governor General may by order—

(a) divide the island into medical districts;

(b) distinguish such districts by such names or numbers as he or she may think proper; and

(c) reduce or increase the number of the districts or alter the boundaries.

### 4. Authority to appoint and transfer Medical Officers and to fix their emoluments

(1) It is lawful for the Governor General to appoint a Chief Medical Officer, a resident medical officer and a surgeon for the Victoria Hospital, and other medical officers for Saint Lucia, and to the districts into which it is divided, and to transfer any medical officer from one district to another, or from the post of resident medical officer to a district or *vice versa.*

(2) The Chief Medical Officer and medical officers hold office during the Governor General's pleasure and receive such emoluments as may be assigned to them by the Governor General and House of Assembly.

(3) The consent of any medical officer to the alteration of the limits of his or her district or to his or her transfer from one district to another is not necessary but he or she shall not suffer any diminution of official salary by reason of the alteration or transfer.

*Duties of Medical Officers*

### 5. Duties of Chief Medical Officer

The Chief Medical Officer is *ex officio* a member of the medical board of Saint Lucia, and has the general superintendence and direction of the public medical service of Saint Lucia and of the several hospitals and medical establishments supported by the Government, and shall perform such other duties as the Governor General may direct.

### 6. Duties of District Medical Officers

The duties of each medical officer within his or her district are as follows—

(a) to afford at the appointed stations on the appointed days and hours, free medical aid to all paupers, to all workers' children under 14 years of age, and to all workers over 60 years.

However, the medical officer shall attend at the residence of a pauper, child, or worker over 60 years of age, who is too ill to travel, or in the case of a child, to be carried to such station;

(b) to afford medical aid to any worker not entitled to gratuitous medical aid under this Act who may require his or her services for such remuneration as is in accordance with the table of fees fixed in the rules to be made by the Governor General under the authority of this Act;

(c) to vaccinate under the provisions of any Act regulating vaccination, and subject to the approval of the Chief Medical Officer to appoint the place and hours for vaccination;

(d) to afford free medical aid to the constables and non-commissioned officers of the Police Force, and at the request of the Officer in charge of any Police Station, or Prison, to afford such aid to any prisoner confined therein; and also at the request of the Commissioner of Police to examine and report on the physical condition and fitness of any applicant for admission to the Police Force.

However, a rural constable shall not be entitled to be afforded such free medical aid unless when called upon for duty or if he or she has been injured whilst on duty;

(e) to attend at such places and times as the Governor General may direct for the purpose of giving medical aid to such persons as under this Act are entitled thereto and to furnish such returns of such attendance and in such form as may be prescribed by the Chief Medical Officer;

(f) to attend and give evidence at all inquests and to make *post mortem* or other examinations when required by the coroner without fee or reward;

(g) to attend at any Government hospital or medical institution daily or more often if necessary or as directed by the Chief Medical Officer and to afford medical aid to all persons who are under treatment and to discharge such duties in connection with such hospital as may be prescribed by the Governor General by rules;

(h) to enter into and inspect any shop, building, stall or place kept or used for the sale of butchers' meat, poultry, fish or any other article of human food or any spirituous or fermented liquors, and to examine any animal, meat, carcase, poultry, fish or any other article of human food or any spirituous or fermented liquor, which may be there and in case any butcher's meat, poultry or fish so examined appears to him or her to be intended but unfit for food to seize and destroy them or cause them to be seized by any constable and destroyed, and in case of any other articles of human food or spirituous or fermented liquor, he or she shall cause the same to be seized and shall proceed against the person to whom they belong or in whose possession they are found. Every such person is liable on conviction to a penalty not exceeding $48 and in default of payment to imprisonment with or without hard labour for any term not exceeding 2 months;

(i) to carry out such duties under the Public Health Act or any Act relating to public health in force as the Chief Medical Officer may direct and to inspect as often as the Chief Medical Officer may direct any part of his or her district, and for the purpose of such inspection it is lawful for him or her, if he or she has good reason to believe that the sanitary condition is not satisfactory to enter into and inspect any house, building, or premises within his or her district, and to inquire into the sanitary condition, reporting to the sanitary authority any nuisance he or she may find existing;

(j) to furnish to the Chief Medical Officer at the end of every month or more often if required a return setting out the sanitary condition of his or her district, especially of the towns and villages, the nature and locality of all nuisances, if any, the date of all visits of sanitary inspection, the names of the places so visited, and such other particulars, if any, as the Chief Medical Officer may require;

(k) to perform if required so to do by the Governor General the duties of health or visiting officer of any port in his or her district under any Act of Saint Lucia regulating such duties;

(l) to give medical aid in cases of serious illness and to any woman in child-birth without demanding prepayment. But this shall not debar him or her from afterwards recovering payment if the person attended to is not entitled to free treatment;

(m) to certify the causes of death of patients dying, as required by any Act now or hereafter to be in force in Saint Lucia regulating the registration of births and deaths, without fee or reward;

(n) at the request of the officer in charge of any police station without fee or reward, and with the necessary consent, to examine and report on the physical condition and fitness of any person in police custody.

### 7. Duties of Officers to Hospitals or Institutions

The Governor General may define the duties of medical officers to hospitals or other Government institutions.

### 8. Residence of Medical Officers

The medical officers shall reside at such places within their respective districts as may be selected or approved by the Governor General.

### 9. When house allowance or value of quarters of District Medical Officers pensionable

Despite anything in the Pensions Act the word “**salary**” shall not, in the case of a district medical officer, be considered to include house allowance or the estimated value of free quarters unless such house allowance or free quarters is mentioned in his or her letter of appointment.

### 10. Officer leaving District without permission

A medical officer shall not leave his or her district for a longer period than 24 hours at any one time without the permission of the Chief Medical Officer, except in the case of great emergency.

*Payment for certain Duties*

### 11. Fees payable by labourers for medical aid

It is not lawful for any medical officer to recover from any worker any remuneration for medical aid except in accordance with the table of fees fixed in the Rules to be made by the Governor General.

### 12. Recovery of fees on deferred payment ticket

A medical officer suing for fees due to him or her under any system of deferred payment provided by the Rules, are not required to pay any Court fees; but if judgment is given against the defendant, the magistrate shall also give judgment against the defendant for the amount of fees which would, except for this enactment, be payable by the plaintiff, and shall pay the same, when recovered, into the Treasury.

*Frauds, etc*

### 13. Obtaining medical aid by false pretences

Whoever by any false representation procures or attempts to procure for himself or herself or for any other person any benefit under this Act or under any of the Rules, commits a summary offence and is liable to a fine of $9.60, or in default of payment to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for 2 months.

### 14. Punishment of persons assaulting Medical Officers while executing their duty

A person who assaults or resists or who aids or incites to assault or resists any medical officer in the execution of any duty imposed on him or her by this Act commits a summary offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $24, and in default of payment to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for 2 months. The provisions of this section shall not bar or hinder a prosecution in any court as for an aggravated assault when the circumstances of the case may justify such prosecution.

### 15. Authority of Medical Officer to sue

The legal interest in all contracts made on behalf of any Government medical institution and in all debts due thereto or in respect thereof is considered vested in the medical officer in charge of such institution who shall have authority to sue for or in respect of the same. However, the sanction of the Governor General for any suit to be brought must first be obtained.

*Rules*

### 16. Power to make Rules

The Governor General may make rules—

(a) for fixing a table of fees to be paid to medical officers for professional services rendered to workers who are not entitled to gratuitous medical aid, and for the payment of such fees;

(b) for the good management, control and government of Government hospitals, asylums and dispensaries; and

(c) generally for the carrying out of the purposes of this Act.
