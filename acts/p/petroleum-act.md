### AN ACT to provide for the importation and to regulate the storage and sale of petroleum and for other connected purposes.

Commencement [29 June 1900]

### 1. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Petroleum Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

“**petroleum**” includes kerosene oil, rock oil, rangoon oil, burmah oil, paraffin oil, and all products of any of them and any oil made from petroleum, coal, schist, shale, peat, or other bituminous substance;

“**volatile petroleum**” means such petroleum as gives off an inflammable vapour at a temperature less than 73 degrees of Fahrenheit's thermometer to be ascertained in the manner mentioned in section 15.

#### Importation

### 3. Importation of volatile petroleum forbidden

It is not lawful to land in, or to import into Saint Lucia or to attempt to land in or import into Saint Lucia any volatile petroleum, and every person who offends against the provision of this section is liable to a penalty not exceeding $240 and in default of payment to imprisonment for a period not exceeding 6 months. Any petroleum so landed or imported or attempted to be landed or imported shall be forfeited and shall be disposed of by the Director of Finance and Planning either by sale for exportation or by destruction of the same, and the proceeds of sale for exportation made by the Director of Finance and Planning under this section shall be paid into the Treasury to the credit of the General Revenue. However, volatile petroleum may be imported, landed, conveyed, kept, sold or used under and in accordance with regulations made by the Governor General.

### 4. Consignee of petroleum for importation to give notice to Director of Finance and Planning

The consignee of any petroleum on board any vessel arriving in Saint Lucia laden or partly laden with petroleum for importation shall within 6 hours (Sundays and holidays excepted) of the arrival of such vessel notify the Director of Finance and Planning of the arrival of the petroleum and of his or her intention to import the same and the Director of Finance and Planning shall thereupon cause not less than 3 samples to be taken of each brand or of each quality of petroleum and shall transmit such samples to such person as the Governor General shall authorise in order that the same may be tested.

Within 24 hours (Sundays and holidays excepted) of the receipt of such samples, the person so authorised shall test the same and shall certify to the Director of Finance and Planning in the Form prescribed by Schedule 1 that the samples so tested by him or her do or do not (as the case may be) consist of volatile petroleum. However, the Director of Finance and Planning may allow oil to be imported into Saint Lucia if he or she is satisfied as to the *bona fides* of any certificate from the authorities of the place from which it has been exported showing that the oil is of a quality not prohibited by this Act.

### 5. Landing and storage of petroleum not being volatile

If the person so authorised certifies that the samples do not consist of volatile petroleum the Director of Finance and Planning shall communicate a copy of such certificate to the owner of the petroleum from which the samples have been taken and shall permit such petroleum to be brought on shore at such place as may be provided for that purpose and landed there and stored in accordance with the regulations.

### 6. Proceeding in case of volatile petroleum

If the person so authorised certifies that the samples do consist of volatile petroleum the Director of Finance and Planning shall communicate a copy of such certificate to the owner of the petroleum from which such samples have been taken and such petroleum shall be considered to all intents and purposes to be volatile petroleum within the meaning of this Act.

### 7. Power to Director of Finance and Planning to test or retest petroleum suspected of being volatile in Warehouse

It is lawful for the Director of Finance and Planning to cause samples to be taken of any petroleum which he or she may have reason to believe to be volatile petroleum stored in the Petroleum Warehouse whether samples of such petroleum have been already tested or not and to cause such samples to be tested and upon such samples being tested the testing officer shall certify to the Director of Finance and Planning that such samples do or do not (as the case may be) consist of volatile petroleum.

### 8. Volatile petroleum in bond to be forfeited and seized by Director of Finance and Planning

If the person so authorised as aforesaid certifies that such samples do consist of volatile petroleum the Director of Finance and Planning shall communicate a copy of such certificate to the owner of the petroleum from which such samples have been taken and such petroleum is considered to all intents and purposes to be volatile petroleum within the meaning of this Act and is liable to be forfeited and seized by the Director of Finance and Planning and disposed of under the provisions of section 3. However if sufficient evidence is produced to satisfy the Director of Finance and Planning that the petroleum was purchased or received on consignment by the party holding it in bond in ignorance of the fact that it was under the required test he or she is permitted to export the same provided he or she does so within 60 days from the date that the same is tested otherwise such purchaser or consignee is not entitled to the benefits of this paragraph.

### 9. Proceeding in case of test being disrupted by owner of petroleum

If the owner of any petroleum certified to be volatile petroleum under sections 4 and 7 is dissatisfied with the test made by the person so authorised under sections 6 and 8 it is lawful for the owner of such petroleum within 24 hours of the receipt of the copy of the certificate from the Director of Finance and Planning to give notice to the Director of Finance and Planning that he or she disputes the correctness of the test of the samples of such petroleum and the owner of the petroleum may there-upon require some other competent person to be approved by the Governor General to make another test of the samples.

### 10. Petroleum certified to be non-volatile on fresh test not to be considered volatile

If the tester nominated as aforesaid certifies that such samples do not consist of volatile petroleum, the petroleum from which such samples is taken despite the provisions of sections 4 and 7 are not considered to be volatile petroleum within the meaning of this Act.

### 11. If petroleum on fresh test is certified as volatile testing fees to be paid by person demanding such test

If the tester nominated as aforesaid certifies that such samples do consist of volatile petroleum, the petroleum from which such samples have been taken is considered to all intents and purposes as volatile petroleum within the meaning of this Act and the fees of the testers shall be paid into the Treasury by the person demanding the fresh test or may be recovered from him or her on the complaint of the Director of Finance and Planning or any person authorised by him or her in writing in that behalf before a magistrate in the same manner as servants' wages.

### 12. Power to Director of Finance and Planning in cases of ships requiring quick despatch

Whenever a vessel arrives at Saint Lucia laden or partly laden with petroleum for importation and requiring quick despatch it is lawful for the Director of Finance and Planning despite the provisions hereinbefore contained to permit such petroleum to be landed and stored in accordance with the regulations.

### 13. Names of persons authorised to test to be published in Gazette

The Governor General shall cause to be published in the Gazette the name of any person authorised to test petroleum; and such person shall on such publication be a person authorised to test petroleum under this Act.

### 14. Remuneration to testers

Every person authorised to test petroleum under this Act is entitled to receive a fee for testing samples of petroleum when called upon to do so, at the rate of $2.40 for testing each set of 3 samples. Such fee shall be paid into the Treasury by the importer and paid by the Director of Finance and Planning to the person entitled thereto in the usual manner on the Certificate of the Director of Finance and Planning.

### 15. Test to be “Abel's Petroleum Testing Apparatus”

The temperature at which petroleum gives off an inflammable vapour shall for the purposes of this Act be tested by Abel's Petroleum Testing Apparatus in the manner set out in Schedule 2.

### 16. Governor General may make rules

It is lawful for the Governor General to make rules and regulations fixing the fees payable for a licence to deal in or sell petroleum, for the landing, storing and delivery of petroleum and as to the vessels in which petroleum is kept for sale by retail and for the taking and keeping of samples of petroleum to be tested under this Act and to protect persons and property against fire or explosion and to determine the restrictions to be imposed upon persons when in close proximity to volatile petroleum stored or being used for any purpose at any place and generally for effectually carrying out the provisions of this Act.

(*Amended by Act 12 of 1961*)

### 17. Penalty

A person who commits a breach of this Act not hereinbefore provided for or a breach of any regulations to be made hereunder is liable for every such offence to a penalty not exceeding $96.

### 18. Exemption of petroleum not used for burning, &c

This Act shall not be held to apply to petroleum not used for burning and imported and kept stored in closed glass bottles not exceeding 12 ounces.

*Sale*

### 19. Prohibition of selling petroleum without licence

(1) A person shall not deal in or sell petroleum without a licence given under this Act, or otherwise than according to the terms of such licence.

(2) A person who contravenes this section is liable on conviction to a penalty not exceeding $96.

### 20. Granting of licence

The Director of Finance and Planning shall grant to any person in Saint Lucia whom he or she considers fit, upon application in writing for that purpose, a licence to sell petroleum. The application shall state the building or part thereof in which petroleum is intended to be kept.

### 21. Duration of licence

The licence may be for one year beginning on 1 January, or for 6 months beginning on 1 January or July, or for 3 months beginning on 1 January, April, July, or October; and every such licence shall mention the building in which petroleum is intended to be kept.

### 22. Appeal to the Governor General from refusal of Director of Finance and Planning to grant licence

(1) If, on any application for a licence under this Act, the Director of Finance and Planning refuses the licence, the Director of Finance and Planning shall, if required by the applicant, certify in writing the grounds on which he or she refused the licence or annexed conditions to the grant thereof, and shall deliver the certificate to the applicant, who may thereupon, within 10 days from the time of the delivery thereof, transmit the same to the Governor General, together with a memorial praying that the licence may be granted despite such refusal.

(2) The Governor General may, on consideration of such memorial and certificate, grant the licence prayed for, either absolutely or with such conditions as may be deemed expedient; and the licence so granted, when certified under the hand of the Governor General, shall be to all intents valid.

### 23. Penalty for keeping, offering or exposing for sale petroleum contrary to provisions of Act

(1) If any petroleum is kept, or is offered or exposed for sale, contrary to the provisions of this Act, the same is liable to be seized by any revenue officer, police officer, or constable, and, upon proof of such keeping, or of such offering or exposing for sale, shall be adjudged by any magistrate to be forfeited, and the person so offending is further liable to a penalty not exceeding $96.

(2) It is lawful for any officer or sergeant of police, or any other person appointed for that purpose by the Director of Finance and Planning or the Commissioner of Police at all reasonable times to inspect and cause to be tested all petroleum kept or offered or exposed for sale. However, if the person in whose possession any petroleum is found which does not stand the test as aforesaid, claims to have a further test made on his or her behalf, the magistrate before whom complaint of the said offence is made shall cause another test to be made by a medical officer, whose certificate on the subject under his or her signature shall be accepted as evidence in any Court of Justice.

#### Storage

### 24. Restriction on quantity of petroleum to be kept otherwise than in the Petroleum Warehouse

(1) Petroleum shall not be kept for sale or otherwise in any larger quantity than 100 gallons, in any one store, shop, or other building, except in such building (to be called the petroleum warehouse) as may be specially appointed for that purpose by the Governor General, and under such conditions as to the mode of storage, as to the nature of goods with which petroleum may be stored, as to testing such petroleum, and as to the safe keeping of petroleum, as may seem expedient to the Governor General.

(2) Petroleum shall not be kept for private consumption in any dwelling-house or premises in any larger quantity than 10 gallons.

(3) Any petroleum kept in contravention of this section shall be forfeited, and in addition thereto the occupier of the place in which the petroleum is kept shall, on being convicted thereof, be liable to a penalty not exceeding $96 for each day during which petroleum is therein kept in contravention of this section, and in addition, if he or she is licensed to sell petroleum, to the forfeiture of his or her licence.

### 25. Tariff of charges for storing petroleum in the Warehouse

It is lawful for the Director of Finance and Planning to prepare a tariff of charges to be paid upon all petroleum stored in the petroleum warehouse, and to alter such tariff; and such tariff or altered tariff, on being approved by the Governor General, is binding on all persons who may deposit petroleum in the said Warehouse. However, the existing tariff shall remain in force until a new tariff is prepared under this Act.

#### Miscellaneous

### 26. Mode of dealing with forfeited petroleum

(1) Any petroleum forfeited, together with the packages containing it, shall be dealt with as the Governor General may direct.

(2) A half of the proceeds of any forfeited petroleum, and a half of any penalty recovered under this Act, shall be paid into the Treasury for the use of Saint Lucia, and the other half in either case shall be paid to the informer, seizing officer, or other officer concerned in the seizure or recovery, in such proportion as may be determined by the Governor General.

### 27. Holder of licence liable for breach of Act by any member of his or her family

The holder of a petroleum licence is liable for the breach of any of the provisions of this Act by any member of his or her family or by any person employed in or about his or her store, shop, or premises.

### 28. Mode of recovery of penalties

All penalties under this Act may be recovered before a district court on the complaint of the Director of Finance and Planning or Commissioner of Police or of any person authorised by them in writing in that behalf and the procedure shall be that for the time being in force regulating procedure in the district courts.

### 29. Exemption of petroleum imported for use of War Department

This Act shall not apply to petroleum imported by the War Department and stored in the War Department Buildings outside of Castries.

## Schedule 2

(Section 16)

### DIRECTIONS FOR PREPARING AND USING APPARATUS FOR TESTING PETROLEUM

The apparatus used for testing Petroleum shall be the Abel Petroleum Testing Apparatus.

*Directions for Drawing the Sample and Preparing it for Testing*

**1.   Drawing The Sample**

In all cases the testing officer or some person duly authorised by him or her shall personally superintend the drawing of the sample from an original unopened tin or other vessel.

An opening sufficiently large to admit of the oil being rapidly poured or siphoned from the tin or other vessel shall be made.

Two bottles each of the capacity of about 40 fluid ounces are to be filled with oil. One of these, the contents of which is intended to be preserved for reference in case of need, is to be carefully corked, the cork being well driven home, cut off level with the neck, and melted sealing-wax worked into it. The other bottle may be either stoppered or corked.

**2.   Preparing The Sample For Testing**

About 10 fluid ounces of the oil, sufficient for 3 tests, are transferred from the bottle into which the sample has been drawn to a pint flask or bottle which is to be immersed in water artificially cooled until a thermometer, introduced into the oil, indicates a temperature not exceeding 50 degrees Fahrenheit.

*Manner of Testing Petroleum*

**3.   Construction Of Apparatus**

The Abel apparatus shall be constructed in accordance with the specification contained in Part I of Schedule 2 to the British Petroleum (Consolidation) Act, 1928 of the United Kingdom and verified and stamped by the Board of Trade in London.

**4.   Placing of Apparatus**

The test apparatus shall be placed for use in a position where it is not exposed to currents of air or draughts.

**5.   Preparing The Heating Vessel Or Water-Bath**

The heating vessel or water-bath shall be filled by pouring water into the funnel until it begins to flow out at the spout of the vessel. The temperature of the water at the beginning of the test shall be 130 degrees Fahrenheit and no heat shall be applied to the water-bath during the test. When a test has been completed and it is desired to make another test the water-bath shall be again raised to 130 degrees Fahrenheit which may conveniently be done while the petroleum cup is being emptied, cooled and refilled with a fresh sample to be tested. The next test is then proceeded with.

**6.   Preparing The Test-Lamp**

If an oil test-lamp is being used it shall be prepared by fitting it with a piece of flat plaited candle-wick, and filling it with colza or rape-oil up to the lower edge of the opening of the spout or wick tube. The lamp shall be trimmed so that when lighted it gives a flame of about 0.15 of an inch diameter, and this size of flame, which is represented by the projecting white bead on the cover of the oil-cup, is readily maintained by simple manipulation from time to time with a small wire trimmer. A gas test-lamp may be employed, and if so, the size of the jet of flame shall be adjusted to the size laid down above.

**7.   Filling The Oil-Cup**

Before the test is begun the temperature of the oil shall be determined and the temperature of the oil-cup shall be brought to approximately 50 degrees Fahrenheit. The oil-cup may be cooled by placing it bottom downwards in water at a temperature not exceeding 50 degrees Fahrenheit, care being taken to prevent any water entering the interior of the cup. The bath having been raised to the proper temperature, the cup shall be placed on a level surface in a good light, and the oil to be tested shall be poured into it, until the level of the liquid just reaches the point of the gauge which is fixed in the cup. The cover, with the slide closed, shall then be put on to the cup and pressed down so that its edge rests on the rim of the cup, and the cup shall be placed into the bath or heating vessel, every care being taken to avoid wetting the sides of the cup with the oil. The thermometer in the lid of the cup has been adjusted so as to have the correct immersion when the brass collar of the thermometer is properly seated, and its position shall not in any circumstances be altered. When the cup has been placed in the proper position, the scale of the thermometer faces the operator.

**8.   Application Of The Test**

The test-lamp shall then be placed in position upon the lid of the cup. When the temperature reaches 66 degrees Fahrenheit the operation of testing shall be begun, the test flame being applied once for every rise of one degree, in the following manner—

The slide shall be slowly drawn open while a metronome, set so as to beat at the rate of 75 to 80 beats in the minute, beats 3 times and shall be closed during the fourth beat. A pendulum of 24 inches effective length may be used in place of the metronome, counting one beat from one extremity of the swing to the other.

**9.   Taking of Flash Point**

The flash point shall be taken as the temperature read on the oil cup thermometer at the time of the flame application which causes a distinct flash in the interior of the oil cup. If a temperature of 73 degrees Fahrenheit has been reached without a flash occurring the testing officer shall certify that the petroleum has a flash point above 73 degrees Fahrenheit and is not “volatile petroleum” within the meaning of the Petroleum Act.
