### AN ACT to regulate the operation and navigation of power-craft and to provide for connected matters.

Commencement [29 February 1964]

### 1. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Power-Craft Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

**“operate”** with its grammatical variations means to drive a power-craft while it is propelled wholly or partly by machinery;

**“power-craft”** means any type of boat capable of a speed of 5 knots or more, used or capable of being used as a means of transportation, and propelled by any form of machinery whether or not such machinery is the principal method of propulsion, but does not include—

(a) Her Majesty's Ships and harbour craft;

(b) ships and craft of all description in the services of any foreign power recognised by Her Majesty;

(c) sea-going vessels engaged on voyages to or from places outside of the Island;

(d) coasting steamers as defined by the Coasting Steamers Act;

(e) ships' lifeboats;

(f) flying-boats, sea-planes, or other types of air-craft capable of landing on the water;

(g) yachts with auxiliary engines; or

(h) any other craft or class of craft as may be exempted by the Governor General under the provisions of regulations made under this Act.

### 3. Restrictions on the operation of power-craft

Without prejudice to any other provision of law relating to the navigation of any craft or class of craft within the territorial sea of this Island, a person shall not within that sea, operate any power-craft—

(a) if he or she is under the age of 16 years; and

(b) unless such power-craft is validly licensed in accordance with the provisions of any law in force for the purpose.

*(Amended by Act 6 of 1984)*

### 4. Restrictions on the use of power-craft, water skis

(1) Any person who operates a power-craft, water-ski, surf-board, water-sleigh or other towed object of a similar nature within the territorial sea of this Island in a manner which is dangerous to navigation or to the life or limb of any other person, having regard to all the circumstances including the nature and condition of that sea and the use that at the time is or might reasonably be expected to be made of that sea, is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $500 and, in default of payment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 6 months.

(2) Any person who operates a power-craft, water-ski, surf-board, water-sleigh or other towed object of a similar nature within the territorial sea of this Island so as to create a nuisance to any other person or boat (whether anchored or not) is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $100 and, in default of payment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding one month.

(3) Any person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of this Island while towing another person on a water-ski, surf-board, water-sleigh or other object of a similar nature when there is not on board the power-craft another person of or above the age of 16 keeping watch on the person being towed is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $200, and in default of payment to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 months.

(4) Any person who, while his or her ability to operate a power-craft is impaired by alcohol or a drug, operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of this Island, commits an offence.

*(Amended by Act 6 of 1984)*

### 5. Procedure in case of accident

(1) Where a power-craft is involved in an accident or collision the person having the care, charge or control of it shall give, on demand, his or her name and address and the number of the licence of the power-craft to any person injured or to the owner of any property damaged or to a police officer or to the port manager; and where as a result of any such accident or collision any other person is injured or appears to required assistance he or she shall offer assistance to that person.

(2) Any accident involving a power-craft shall be reported by the person operating the same at the time of the accident to a police officer or to the port manager within 12 hours thereof.

### 6. Additional punishment for breaches

When a person is convicted of an offence for a violation or section 4 the Court may, in addition to any other punishment that may be imposed for that offence, make all or any of the following orders—

(a) prohibit him or her from operating a power-craft within the territorial sea of this Island for a period not exceeding one year;

(b) cancel the licence relating to the power-craft for such period not exceeding one year as to the Court may seem proper.

*(Amended by Act 6 of 1984)*

### 7. Operating power-craft while prohibited

Any person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of this Island while he or she is prohibited from operating a power-craft or while the licence with respect to the power-craft operated by him or her has been cancelled by reason of an order made under section 8 is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $500 and in default of payment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 6 months.

*(Amended by Act 6 of 1984)*

### 8. Punishment where no other penalty is provided

A person who—

(a) commits an offence against this Act; or

(b) contravenes or fails to comply with any of the provisions of this Act,

for which no other penalty is provided is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $100 and, in default of payment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding one month.

### 9. Power to arrest

Without prejudice to any other provision of law authorising arrest without warrant, a police officer may arrest without warrant any person operating, or within, a power-craft whom he or she has reason to suspect of having committed an offence against section 4 or who has failed to give his name and address or the number of the licence of a power-craft in accordance with, and in the circumstances mentioned in section 5(1).

### 10. Power to board power-craft and require information

(1) Where a police officer has reason to suspect that an offence has been committed against any provision of this Act or of regulations made thereunder it is lawful for him or her—

(a) to board the power-craft concerned and address such pertinent enquiries to the occupants thereof as he or she may consider necessary;

(b) to require the owner of the power-craft concerned to give all information in his or her possession as to the name, address, description and whereabouts of the operator and of the occupants of the power-craft at the time of the suspected offence.

(2) Every person who fails to give information required by a police officer in accordance with the preceding subsection commits an offence.

### 11. Proof of speed

In any proceedings under this Act the burden of proving that a power-craft was not capable of a speed of 5 knots shall lie on the person alleging the same.

### 12. Powers of governor general

The Governor General may by regulations made under this Act—

(a) regulate the navigation, operation and conduct of power-craft;

(b) provide for the licensing of power-craft under this Act *in lieu* of licences under any other Act and for the fees to be paid for such licences;

(c) provide for the exemption of any craft or class of craft from the provisions of this Act; and

(d) provide generally for the better carrying out of the purposes of this Act,

and may provide penalties, not exceeding a fine of $100 and in default of payment imprisonment for a term not exceeding one month, for the breach or non-observance of such regulations.
