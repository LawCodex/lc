### AN ACT to regulate and impose duties on the brewing of beer.

Commencement [10 March 1976]

### 1. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Brewery Act.

### 2. Interpretation

(1) In this Act—

“**approved**” means approved by the Comptroller;

“**beer**” includes ale, porter, spruce beer, and blackbeer and any other description of beer, and any liquor which is made or sold as a description of beer or as a substitute for beer, and which on analysis of a sample thereof at any time shall be found to contain more than 2% of proof spirit, or to have an original gravity exceeding 1012 degrees. However, the Minister has power to modify this definition or permit such exemptions as may be found desirable;

“**brewer**” means a brewer of beer for sale, that is to say, any person who brews beer for the use of any other person at any place other than the premises of the person for whose use the beer shall be brewed, and any person licensed to deal in or retail beer, who brews beer;

“**Comptroller**” means the Comptroller of Customs and Excise;

“**Minister**” means the Minister to whom the responsibility for customs and excise has been assigned;

“**officer**” means the Comptroller or any Supervisor or any officer of the Customs and Excise Department designated by the Comptroller;

“**original gravity**” means the gravity of beers, maltas or malta liquors before fermentation has commenced;

“**other malt beverage**” means any non-alcoholic brewed malt beverage or such beverage containing less than 2% of proof spirit or any liquor sold under the description of malta, malt beverage or malt liquor;

“**prepared grist**” means rice, and any other description of corn which may have been subjected to any special process, other than malting, for use in brewing;

“**required**” means required by the comptroller;

“**sugar**” means any saccharine substance, extract, or syrup, and includes any material capable of being used in brewing except malt or corn;

“**State**” means the State of Saint Lucia;

“**wort**” means any extract or solution convertible into beer.

(2) The provisions of this Act, except section 3(5) shall apply with the necessary modifications to other malt beverage as they apply to beer.

### 3. Licence to brew

(1) There shall be paid to the Comptroller for the use of the State a duty of $2,500 on a licence to be taken out annually by a brewer in the State.

(2) Every such licence shall be in such a form as the Comptroller shall direct, and shall, whenever issued, be granted only on payment of the duty in full, and shall expire on 31 December in each year.

(3) Such licence shall not authorise the brewer to sell beer other than brewed by himself or herself, and the quantity to be sold at any one time shall not be less than one gallon.

(4) If any person brews for sale without having in force a proper licence under this Act, he or she is liable to a fine of $2,500 and all wort, beer, vessels, utensils, and materials for brewing shall be forfeited.

(5) If any brewer sells any quantity of beer less than one gallon he or she is deemed to be a retailer and is liable to any penalty for retailing beer without a licence.

### 4. Charges on brewery products

Subject to section 11 any duty, tax or levy on products manufactured at a brewery and consumed locally shall be charged, levied and collected in accordance with any law in force in Saint Lucia that requires that duty, tax or levy be paid. *(Inserted by Act 14 of 2000)*

### 5. Maximum Alcohol content of beer

Beer shall not be brewed containing more than 10% of pure ethyl alcohol by volume.

### 6. Beer to be deposited in an approved warehouse

All beer produced by a brewer for sale shall be deposited in an approved beer warehouse and beer, beer worts or solution shall not be delivered from the entered brewery premises, other than for direct warehousing, save as may be allowed on authority of the Comptroller.

### 7. Duty to be charged on volume

Duty on beer and malta or malt liquor brewed by a brewer for sale shall be charged on the volume in gallons released from the brewery and on such other quantities as are not satisfactorily accounted for by the brewer.

### 8. Duty to be paid on deficiency

(1) Duty shall be payable on any deficiency in the production of beer which cannot be shown to the Comptroller's satisfaction to have arisen due to natural process or accidental loss or spoilage during the process.

(2) The assessment of a deficiency under the regulation shall be by reference to the material used in the production of beer and every brewer for sale shall be deemed to have brewed 43 gallons of beer of original gravity 1055o for every 100 lbs. of malt and also 65 gallons of beer of original gravity 1055o for every 100 lbs. of sugar in the brewing records or used by him or her.

### 9. Mode of ascertaining gravity

(1) An approved sacharometer and tables shall be used to ascertain the quantity by relation to gravity of all wort, and in calculating the gravity a degree of gravity shall be taken as equal to 1/1000 of the gravity of distilled water at 68o Fahrenheit.

(2) The quantity and gravity so ascertained shall be deemed to be the true quantity and gravity of such wort.

(3) Where for the purpose of this Act it is necessary to ascertain the original gravity of beer or wort to which yeast has been added, the original gravity shall be determined in such manner as may be approved by the Comptroller and prescribed in regulations made under this Act.

### 10. Collection by Comptroller

The collection of any duty, tax or levy under this Act shall be the responsibility of the Comptroller.

### 11. Regulation

(1) The Minister may make regulations as may be necessary for ascertainment, charging, levying and collection of the duties charged levied and to be collected.

(2) Regulations made under this section may prescribe in respect of any contravention thereof or failure to comply therewith a penalty of $500 on summary conviction.

(3) Regulations purporting to be made under this section shall be published in the Gazette and shall come into operation on such publication or at such other time as may be stated in such regulation.

### 12. Destruction of spoilt beer

Beer, beer wort or solution found to be unfit for consumption before removal from the brewery premises or approved warehouse may be destroyed without payment of duty, if prior application is made to the officer.

### 13. Qualification as to remission of duty on spoilt beer

Every brewer for sale claiming remission or repayment of duty in respect of beer brewed by him or her which has been removed from his or her entered premises and which has accidentally become spoilt or otherwise unfit for consumption shall deliver to the proper officer an application together with a statement in writing giving—

(a) such detail as will enable it to be identified to a specific delivery from the approved warehouse;

(b) a declaration by himself or herself or by some responsible person in his or her employment that—

(i) none of the beer referred to in the statement has borne a charge of duty less than that chargeable at the minimum rate of duty in force at the time the beer was brewed,

(ii) the beer became accidentally spoilt or otherwise unfit for consumption whilst in the possession of the person to whom it was originally consigned,

(iii) to the best of the declarant's knowledge and belief the beer contains no added substance other than finings for the purpose of clarification,

(iv) no part of the beer consists of waste beer or bottom other than bottoms forming naturally in the beer in the casks or other vessels in which it has been returned, and

(v) the declarant holds a certificate furnished by each consignor specifying the particulars of the spoilt beer returned.

### 14. Brewer to maintain Book in approved form

(1) A book in the approved form shall be maintained by the brewer and the following provisions shall have effect in relation to the book, and to the entries to be made therein—

(a) the brewer shall keep the book in an approved place in his or her entered premises at all times ready for the inspection of the officer, and shall permit any officer at any time to inspect the same and make extracts therefrom;

(b) the brewer shall enter separately in the book the quantity of the several materials which he or she intends to use in his or her next brewing, and also the day and hour when such next brewing is intended to take place;

(c) the brewer shall make such entry, so far as respects the day and hour of brewing, 24 hours at least before he or she shall begin to mash or dissolve, and, so far as respects the quantity, 2 hours at the least before the hour entered for the mashing and the dissolving respectively;

(d) the brewer shall within one hour of the wort being all removed from the copper or boiling vessel prior to cooling record the quantity and original gravity of wort corrected to 68o Fahrenheit and the number and description of the vessel or vessels in which the cold wort will be collected;

(e) the brewer shall cancel the entry not less than 2 hours before the hour entered when he or she does not intend to brew or dissolve on the day and hour entered;

(f) the brewer shall, if so required by the Comptroller send notice in writing containing the required particulars to the proper officer 48 hours before his or her next brewing is intended to take place.

(2) For any contravention of this section the brewer is liable to a fine of $1,000.

### 15. Cancellations or alterations in Brewing Book

Except as may be allowed under section 12 the brewer shall not cancel, obliterate or alter any entry in the brewing record except with the permission of the proper officer.

### 16. Marking of vessels and rooms

(1) Every brewer shall cause to be legibly painted with oil colour, and keep so painted, on some conspicuous part of every mash tun, underback, wort receiver, copper heating tank, cooler and collecting and fermenting vessel intended to be used by him or her in his or her business, and on the outside of the door of every room and place wherein any part of his or her business is to be carried on, the name or initial letter or letters of the name of the vessel, room, or place, according to the purpose for which it is intended.

(2) When more than one vessel, room, or place is used for the same purpose, all such vessels, rooms or places shall be marked by progressive numbers.

(3) All mash tuns, underbacks, wort receivers, copper, heating tanks, cooler, and collecting and fermenting vessels, shall be so placed and fixed as to admit of the contents being accurately ascertained by gauge or measure and shall not be altered in shape, position, or capacity without 2 days previous notice in writing to the officer.

(4) For any contravention of this section the brewer is liable to a fine of $1,000.

### 17. Application for approval of processes vessels and rooms

A brewer shall not begin to brew beer for sale or prepare solution until—

(a) he or she has made written application to the Comptroller for approval of the processes and of all the rooms, places, vessels and plant which he or she intends to use in the brewing of beer or preparation of solution;

(b) the Comptroller has approved such processes, premises, vessels and plant, subject to such conditions as he or she may prescribe.

### 18. Entry of premises

Every brewer shall, before he or she begins to brew, make entry in the required form of all premises, rooms, places and vessels intended to be used by him or her for his or her business, specifying the purpose for which each room, place and vessel is to be used and the mark by which it is distinguished. The brewer shall sign the entry, and deliver it to the officer.

### 19. Record of materials used in brewing of beer

Every brewer shall maintain, in a form agreed with the Comptroller, a record of the receipts, usings, and stocks of materials used in the brewing of beer or preparation of solution, and shall make such entries therein not later than 24 hours after such receipts or usings and shall allow the officer to inspect such a record at all reasonable times and to make entries therein and take extracts therefrom.

### 20. prohibition against adulteration of beer

(1) A brewer shall not adulterate beer, or add any matter or thing thereto (except finings for the purpose of clarification, water or any other matter or thing sanctioned by the Comptroller) before the same is delivered for consumption, and any beer found to be adulterated or mixed with any other matter or thing (except as aforesaid) in the possession of a brewer shall be forfeited, and the brewer is liable to a fine of $1,000.

(2) A dealer in or retailer of beer shall not adulterate or dilute beer, or add any matter or thing thereto (except finings for the purpose of clarification) and any beer found to be adulterated or diluted or mixed with any other matter or thing (except finings) in the possession of a dealer in or a retailer of beer shall be forfeited, and he or she is liable to a fine of $1,000.

### 21. Power to take samples

(1) Any officer may take such samples as he or she may deem necessary of any wort or beer or materials for brewing in possession of any brewer.

(2) The brewer may, if he or she wishes, before any such sample is taken stir up and mix together all such wort, beer or materials from which the sample is taken.

(3) Any officer may at any time take samples of any goods or commodities chargeable with any duty of Excise and Customs.

### 22. Brewer to provide and maintain scales weights and other appliances

(1) Every brewer shall provide and maintain sufficient and just scales and weights, and other necessary and reasonable appliances, to enable the officers to take account of, or check by weight, gauge, or measure all materials and liquids used or intended to be used or produced in brewing.

(2) He or she shall also render all necessary assistance to the officer in the taking of such accounts.

(3) He or she shall also, if required by the officer, provide sufficient lights, ladders, and other conveniences.

(4) For every contravention of this section the brewer shall be liable to a fine of $250.

### 23. Penalty for using false scales or weights

If any person exercising or carrying on a trade or business under or subject to any law of excise and required to keep scales or weights or measures—

(a) in the weight of his or her stock or any goods, uses or suffers to be used any false, unjust or insufficient scales or weight or measure with intent to defraud the revenue of any duty of excise or customs; or

(b) before or after the weighing of his or her stock or any goods, puts or suffers to be put any other substance thereto, whereby any officer may be hindered or prevented from taking a just and true account,

he or she shall for every such offence, be liable to a fine of $1,000, and the false, unjust or insufficient scales, weights and measures shall be forfeited.

### 24. Officer's power of access

(1) An officer may at any time, either by day or by night, enter any part of the premises of a brewer to take an account of the materials used or to be used in brewing and the wort and beer produced.

(2) If an officer, after having demanded admission into the premises of a brewer and declared his or her name and business, is not immediately admitted, the officer and any person acting in his or her aid, may at any time, either by day or night (but at night only in the presence of a police officer) break open any door or window of the premises or break through any wall thereof, for the purpose of obtaining admission, and the brewer is liable to a fine of $2,500.

### 25. Power to enter and search for concealed pipes or vessels

(1) If any officer has reason to suspect that any private or concealed pipe, or conveyance, or vessel, is kept or made use of by a brewer, he or she may, either by day or night (but at night only in the presence of a police officer) break open any part of the premises of such brewer and forcibly enter therein, and may break up the ground in or adjoining such premises, or any wall thereof, to search for such private or concealed pipe or conveyance or vessel.

(2) If such officer shall find any such pipe or conveyance, he or she may enter in any house in the possession of any other person into which such pipe or conveyance may lead, and may break up any part of such house or premises, to search for the vessel communicating with such pipe, and seize and remove such pipe or conveyance or vessel.

(3) Every such pipe, conveyance or vessel, and all beer, wort or materials for brewing found therein shall be absolutely forfeited, and the brewer is liable to a fine of $5,000.

(4) If any damage is done in the search, and such search is unsuccessful, the damage shall be made good.

### 26. Obstruction of officer

If any person himself or herself, or by any person in his or her employ who—

(a) obstructs, hinders, molests or assaults any officer in the performance of any duty under this Act or the exercise of any power imposed or conferred on him or her by or under any enactment relating to Customs;

(b) does anything which impedes or is calculated to impede the carrying out of any entry or the search for anything liable to seizure removal and forfeiture, under this Act or any enactment relating to Customs;

(c) rescues, damages or destroys anything liable to forfeiture under this Act or any enactment relating to customs, or does anything calculated to prevent the procuring or giving of evidence as to whether or not anything is so liable to forfeiture; or

(d) prevents the detention of any person by a person duly engaged or acting as aforesaid, or rescues any person so detained,

or who attempts to do any of the aforementioned things, is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $1,000 or to a term not exceeding 6 months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 27. Use of noxious substance

(1) Whenever it appears to the satisfaction of the Minister that any substance or liquor, is capable of being used in the manufacture or preparation for sale of any article subject to a duty of Customs or Excise, and that such substance or liquor is of a noxious or detrimental nature or, being a chemical or artificial extract or product, may affect prejudicially the interests of the revenue, it shall be lawful for the Minister by notice in the Gazette, to prohibit the use of such substance or liquor in the manufacture or preparation for sale of any article specified in the notice, and by like notice to withdraw such prohibition at any time.

(2) If, after the publication of any such notice of prohibition in the Gazette, any person shall use the substance or liquor thereby prohibited in the manufacture or preparation for sale of any article therein specified, he or she is liable to a fine of $1,000, and any such substance or liquor found in the possession of any person licensed for the manufacture or preparation whereof any such substance or liquor may have been used, shall be forfeited.

### 28. Duty free admission of materials for brewing

All materials imported by brewers for using in brewing and admitted as such by the Comptroller shall be free of all duty whatsoever thereon imposed by any law of the State.

However, any brewer who disposes of any materials admitted free of duty otherwise than for brewing of beer is liable to a fine of $1,000 in addition to the amount of duty payable on such material.

### 29. Jurisdiction

All offences under this Act may be prosecuted, and all penalties incurred may be imposed or recovered in a court of summary jurisdiction.
