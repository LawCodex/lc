### AN ACT to provide an independent and accessible review mechanism for users of health services, to provide a means for reviewing and improving the quality of health service provision and for related matters.

Commencement [1 July 2002]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Health Services (Complaints and Conciliation) Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

“**Commission**” means the Health Services (Complaints and Conciliation) Commission established under section 3;

“**confidential information**” means information gained as a result of a person's position, or employment with or as a health service provider;

“**health service**” includes—

(a) medical, hospital and nursing service;

(b) dental service;

(c) psychiatric service;

(d) pharmaceutical service;

(e) ambulance service;

(f) community health service;

(g) environmental health service;

(h) service provided by an audiologist, osteopath, optometrist, or occupational therapist, or a chiropodist, chiropractor, dietician physiotherapist, herbalist, homeopathist, or reflexologist;

(i) service provided by a practitioner of naturopathy, acupuncture or in an alternative health care field; and

(j) a service prescribed as a health service for purposes of this Act;

“**health service provider**” includes—

(a) a person or body providing a health service;

(b) a person who manages a health care institution;

(c) any clinic, public hospital, private hospital, community health centre, psychiatric hospital;

(d) the chief executive officer or person holding a similar position of any body listed in paragraph (c); and

(e) a person or organisation that is prescribed as a health service provider for purposes of this Act or that is included in a class of persons or organisations prescribed as health service providers for purposes of this Act;

“**health service worker**” means a person employed by a health service provider and whose duties are connected with the provision of a health service;

“**industrial tribunal**” means any tribunal established under a law in force in Saint Lucia specifically to deal with industrial relations matters;

“**Minister**” means Minister responsible for health;

“**position**” means—

(a) position as a member of the Commission;

(b) position as an officer or employee of the Commission;

(c) position as a participant in an investigation under this Act;

(d) position as a person or body to whom the Commission refers a complaint;

(e) position as a person who has received a notice under section 12;

“**prescribed**” means prescribed in regulations made under section 16;

“**registration authority**” means a body set up under a law for the purpose of registering practitioners in the medical, dental, nursing fields, or other persons entitled to offer a health service;

“**regulations**” means regulations made under section 16;

“**user**” means a person who uses or receives a health service but does not mean a person who has arranged a health service for another.

### 3. Establishment and constitution of Commission

(1) There is hereby established a Health Services (Complaints and Conciliation) Commission to be appointed by the Cabinet and comprising the following members—

(a) a retired judge of the High Court or an attorney-at-law of at least 10 years standing at the Bar;

(b) a medical practitioner of at least 10 years standing or retired medical practitioner nominated by the Saint Lucia Medical and Dental Association;

(c) a nurse of at least 10 years standing or a retired nurse nominated by the Saint Lucia Nurses Association; and

(d) two persons experienced in matters of public administration who shall—

(i) be nominated by the Governor-General,

(ii) represent the public interest, and

(iii) not be serving officers in the public service.

(2) The Cabinet shall appoint one of the members mentioned in subsection (1) to be the Chairperson.

(3) A member of the Commission shall hold office for a term, not exceeding 3 years, specified in his or her letter of appointment, and shall be eligible for re-appointment.

(4) A member of the Commission may resign his or her position in writing addressed to the Chairperson and the Chairperson may resign in writing addressed to the Secretary to Cabinet.

(5) During the temporary absence or incapacity of a member of the Commission the Cabinet may appoint a person selected in accordance with subsection (1) to act in place of the absent or incapacitated member.

(6) A member of the Commission may be removed from his or her office by the Cabinet if—

(a) there is a conflict of interest on the part of the member; or

(b) where there is a withdrawal of support by the body that nominated that member; or

(c) where it is in the public interest to do so.

### 4. Functions of Commission

The Commission shall have the following functions—

(a) to investigate and make recommendations on complaints relating to health services;

(b) to conciliate between users and providers of health services;

(c) to investigate any matter relating to the health services referred to it by the Minister;

(d) to review and to identify the causes of complaints and to make recommendations on ways of minimizing or removing those causes;

(e) to record all complaints received by the Commission in a central register of complaints and to make copies of such register available, upon request, to the Minister or a person authorised by the Minister.

### 5. General powers of Commission

The Commission may—

(a) develop and recommend new procedures for dealing with complaints relating to health services; and

(b) advise health service providers, users and the Minister generally on any matter relating to complaints.

### 6. Powers of investigation

In conducting an investigation the Commission—

(a) subject to section 16(1), may adopt its own procedure;

(b) may exclude the public and media from its sittings;

(c) shall proceed with as little formality and technicality and with as much expedition as the requirements of this Act and proper investigation of the matter permit;

(d) is not bound by the rules of evidence but may inform itself of any matter in such a manner as it considers appropriate;

(e) is bound by the rule of natural justice;

(f) may—

(i) summon a person to attend meetings of the Commission and to answer questions put to him or her by members of the Commission,

(ii) request certified copies of documents, records, reports and statements relevant to a complaint from a health service provider, the Minister or a person making a complaint provided that a person's medical records shall not be requested by or provided to the Commission except with that person's consent in writing,

(iii) request information from health service providers and users concerning the working of the health services complaints system,

(iv) permit an interested person to appear before it to be represented by Counsel or another person in any proceedings under this Act.

### 7. Immunity

A member of the Commission is not liable to any action suit or other proceedings in respect of an act done or omitted to be done in good faith in the exercise of a power conferred or a duty imposed under this Act.

### 8. Persons entitled to complain

(1) A user or his or her representative may complain to the Commission in respect of any of the matters under section 9.

(2) The Commission may recognise as a user's representative a person not chosen by the user and may receive a complaint from that person on the user's behalf if in the Commission's opinion—

(a) the user is unable to complain; or

(b) the user is unable to choose a representative; and

(c) the person who wishes to represent the user has a sufficient interest in the subject matter of the complaint.

(3) A health service worker may complain to the Commission in respect of any matter under section 9.

(4) A person who complains to the Commission may at any time withdraw the complaint by notifying the Commission and the Commission shall determine whether or not to terminate its investigation.

### 9. Grounds of complaints

A person qualified to complain under section 8 may complain on grounds that a health service provider has acted unreasonably—

(a) by not providing a health service to a user;

(b) in the manner of providing a health service to a user;

(c) by denying or restricting the user's access to records kept by the health service provider and relating to a user;

(d) in disclosing a user's health records; or

(e) by not properly investigating, or not taking proper action upon, a complaint made to a health service provider by a user about a health service provider's action which is of a kind listed in paragraph (a) to (d).

### 10. Manner of complaining

(1) A person may complain to the Commission in person or in writing.

(2) A person who complains to the Commission must give his or her name to the Commission and such other information relating to the person's identity as the Commission may require.

(3) If the Commission receives a complaint in person the Commission shall require the person who made it to confirm it in writing signified by the mark of that person unless the person satisfies the Commission that there is good reason why the complaint should not or cannot be confirmed in writing.

(4) The Commission may ask a person who complains to the Commission to give more information about the complaint within a time fixed by the Commission.

(5) A person required to appear before, or to produce documents to the Commission during the investigation of a complaint may be represented by another person.

### 11. Procedure following receipt of complaint

(1) Within 28 days after receiving a complaint or within a further period fixed under subsection (3), the Commission shall decide whether, and to what extent—

(a) to accept it;

(b) to reject it; or

(c) to refer it to a registration authority or to a person, organisation or agency.

(2) To enable the Commission to make a decision under sub-section (1) the Commission may make such inquiries as the Commission considers appropriate.

(3) If the Commission considers that—

(a) a complaint is unduly complex; or

(b) a complaint can be satisfactorily resolved within a further period fixed by the Commission,

the Commission may fix a further period of not more than 28 days for the purposes of subsection (2).

(4) The Commission shall reject a complaint which in the Commission's opinion is frivolous, vexatious, and trivial or does not warrant investigation.

(5) If an issue raised in a complaint has already been determined by a court, an industrial tribunal or a registration authority the Commission shall reject the complaint to the extent to which it relates to that issue unless in the Commission's opinion it relates to matters which were not raised before the court, industrial tribunal or registration authority.

(6) The Commission shall reject a complaint about an incident which occurred more than 12 months before the complaint is made if in the Commission's opinion the person who made the complaint has not shown good reason for the delay.

(7) The Commission may reject a complaint if, when asked, the person who complained to the Commission does not—

(a) confirm the complaint in writing;

(b) give more information about the complaint within the time fixed by the Commission; or

(c) give the Commission more information concerning the person's identity.

(8) The Commission shall investigate a complaint if it is satisfied that the user has taken all reasonable steps, or, if the complaint was made on the user's behalf, all reasonable steps have been taken on the user's behalf, to resolve the matter with the health service provider.

(9) If a complaint raises issues which, in the Commission's opinion, require investigation by another person, organisation or agency, the Commission may refer the complaint to the appropriate person, organisation or agency.

### 12. Procedure for investigation of complaint

(1) Subject to subsection (2), within 14 days after deciding to accept a complaint the Commission shall give written notice, in the form prescribed, of the details of the complaint to the health service provider concerned.

(2) Where the Commission is of the opinion that if a notice or particular information in a notice under this section is given to a health service provider the health or safety of the user may be put at risk, or the proper investigation of the complaint would be prejudiced, the Commission shall not comply with subsection (1) or shall in giving a notice under that subsection omit the particular information.

(3) If subsection (2) applies, the Commission shall give the notice or the particular information to the health service provider without delay after the Commission is satisfied that—

(a) the risk is at an end; or

(b) there is no further likelihood of prejudice to the proper investigation of the complaint,

but in any case not later than 6 months after the complaint was made or, if the investigation ends before that time, before the end of the investigation.

(4) In deciding whether the health service provider has acted unreasonably in the manner set out in the complaint, the Commission shall consider any failure by the health service provider to follow any guiding principles laid down for health service providers of that kind and the extent to which that failure is also a failure to meet what are, in the Commission's opinion, generally accepted standards for health care delivery by health service providers of that kind.

(5) If in the Commission's opinion the person who made the complaint and the health service provider cannot resolve the complaint and after investigating the complaint the Commission decides that the complaint is justified, the Commission shall decide what action should be taken to remedy the complaint.

(6) The Commission shall decide, in a manner that does not conflict with the provisions of any law in force of Saint Lucia, whether a complaint is justified or what action is to be taken.

(7) Within 14 days after deciding whether or not a complaint is justified the Commission shall give written notice of the decision to the user, the Minister and the health service provider.

(8) A notice given under subsection (7) shall include the reason for the decision and shall instruct the health service provider to take the action if any which, the Commission determines necessary to remedy the complaint.

(9) Within 45 days after a health service provider receives notice under subsection (8), or before the end of any extension of time granted under subsection (10) by the Commission, the health service provider shall report in writing to the Commission about what action the health service provider has taken upon the complaint.

(10) Where a health service provider requests an extension of time the Commission may extend the time within which the health service provider must report under subsection (9) by no more than 15 days.

(11) Despite the other provisions of this section the Commission shall after the investigation of a complaint make recommendation to the appropriate registration body, Minister or Director of Public Prosecution as appropriate.

(12) A registration body shall act in accordance with the recommendations of the Commission made under subsection (11) unless it is a contravention of enactment establishing the registration authority to so do.

### 13. Termination of proceedings

(1) The Commission shall terminate proceedings on an issue raised in a complaint—

(a) if the Commission becomes aware that the health service provider, user or a person who made the complaint has begun legal proceedings which relate to that issue;

(b) if the Commission becomes aware that proceedings relating to that specific issue have been initiated before an industrial tribunal; or

(c) subject to subsection (2), if the Commission considers that the issue should properly be dealt with by a court or a registration authority.

(2) The Commission shall not terminate proceedings on a complaint about a health service provider—

(a) which the Commission has referred to the appropriate registration authority if the registration authority asks the Commission to continue dealing with the matter; or

(b) if the Minister has referred the matter to the Commission.

(3) Within 14 days after terminating proceedings in respect of a complaint under subsection (1) the Commission shall give written notice of that fact to the user and the health service provider.

(4) If the Commission terminated proceedings on an issue raised in a complaint and later becomes aware that the user or the health service provider has withdrawn the proceedings under subsection (1)(a), (1)(b) or (1)(c) relating to the complaint, the Commission may, with the consent of the user or the person who complained to the Commission, re-open proceedings under this Act.

### 14. Appeal

(1) An appeal against the decision of the Commission lies to a single judge in Chambers or to a master of the High Court.

(2) The decision of a judge or master under subsection (1) is final.

### 15. Offences and penalties

(1) A person shall not—

(a) by threats or intimidation persuade or attempt to persuade another person not to complain to the Commission or not to continue discussion with or proceedings before the Commission; or

(b) refuse to employ or dismiss another person or subject another person to any detriment, because the other person intends to complain or has complained, to the Commission, or intends to take part, is taking part, or has taken part, in discussions with or proceedings before the Commission.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000 or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding 2 years.

(3) A person who holds or has held a position on the Commission shall not, except to the extent necessary for the purposes of this Act or any other law in force in Saint Lucia, record, disclose, communicate or make use of confidential information obtained while serving as such member.

(4) A person who contravenes subsection (3) commits an offence and upon summary conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $5,000 or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding 2 years.

### 16. Regulations

(1) The Minister may make regulations with respect to—

(a) the procedure of the Commission in carrying out the Commission's functions and powers;

(b) complaints, reports, returns, notices and information provided for in this Act;

(c) prescribing forms;

(d) generally prescribing any matter or thing required or permitted by this Act to be prescribed or necessary to be prescribed to give effect to this Act.

(2) The regulations made under subsection (1)—

(a) may be of general or limited application;

(b) may differ according to differences in time, place or circumstances;

(c) may confer or impose powers, discretions or duties on the Commission, an employee in the public service or any other person; and

(d) may apply, adopt or incorporate by reference a code of practice for health service providers.
