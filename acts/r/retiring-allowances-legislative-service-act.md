### AN ACT to provide retiring allowances and gratuities for legislators and other related matters.

Commencement [1 January 1998]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Retiring Allowances (Legislative Service) Act.

### 2. Interpretation

(1) In this Act—

“**appropriate Minister**” means the Minister responsible for Finance;

“**award**” means an amount payable to a person out of the Consolidated Fund under this Act;

“**legislative term**” means the period commencing in the year 1954 from the date of a general election and expiring at the date of the next ensuing dissolution of Parliament;

“**legislator**” means a person who has served or who is serving as a member of the House of Assembly or of the Senate;

“**Minister**” when not preceded by the word “appropriate” means a legislator appointed as Minister under section 60 of the Constitution of Saint Lucia and, in relation to any period between 1 March 1956 and 22 February 1979 means a legislator duly appointed as a Minister during that period;

“**retiring allowance**” means an allowance payable under section 5;

“**salary**” means the emoluments provided for in the Estimates of Revenue and Expenditure of Saint Lucia exclusive of any allowances.

### 3. Eligibility for gratuity

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, a legislator—

(a) who has served as a Minister for a period or periods which amount in the aggregate to not less than 4 years;

(b) who has served as Speaker of the House of Assembly, President of the Senate, Parliamentary Secretary or Leader of the Opposition for a period or periods in the aggregate of not less than 4 years, and

who has ceased to be a Minister or to hold an office described in paragraph (b) before or after the commencement of this Act shall be paid a gratuity at a rate of 25% of the aggregate of the salary payable to that legislator throughout the period served or the period specified in section 4(2).

(2) The gratuity payable under this section shall be paid without prejudice to any other award to which a person is entitled under this Act.

### 4. Payment of gratuity on death of legislator

(1) Where a legislator who would have been entitled to a gratuity under section 3 or who had opted for a gratuity under section 9 dies before the gratuity has been paid, payment shall be made—

(a) to the legal representative of that legislator; or

(b) where the legislator has no legal personal representative, to the dependants of the legislator as the appropriate Minister thinks fit.

(2) Despite section 3, where a legislator dies while serving as a member of the House of Assembly or of the Senate and at the date of the legislator's death the legislator did not qualify for the payment of a gratuity under section 3, an amount equal to the gratuity that would have been payable in respect of the period specified under that section shall be paid—

(a) to the legal personal representative of the legislator; or

(b) where the legislator has no legal personal representative, to the dependants of the legislator as the appropriate Minister thinks fit.

### 5. Retiring allowance

Subject to the provisions of this Act, a retiring allowance shall be paid to a legislator who—

(a) has served as a member of the House of Assembly, or Senate for periods equal in the aggregate to not less than 8 years, or 2 or more legislative terms of not less than 3 years;

(b) has ceased to serve as a member of the House of Assembly or Senate; and

(c) has attained the age of 50 years, or not having attained the age of 50 years has produced medical evidence to the satisfaction of the appropriate Minister, that he or she is not capable by reason of infirmity of mind or body of discharging the duties of a legislator and that the infirmity is likely to be permanent.

### 6. Effect of dissolution of Parliament

For the purposes of section 5—

(a) a person does not cease to be a legislator by reason only of the dissolution of Parliament;

(b) a legislator who, immediately before the dissolution of Parliament was a member of the House of Assembly or the Senate shall, if that legislator does not become a member of the House of Assembly or the Senate after the general elections next following the dissolution, cease to be a member of the House of Assembly or the Senate from the date of the dissolution.

### 7. Rates of retiring allowance

(1) In this section, Prime Minister means a legislator appointed as a—

(a) Chief Minister or Premier before the coming into operation of the Constitution of Saint Lucia; or

(b) a Prime Minister appointed under section 60 of the Constitution of Saint Lucia.

(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act—

(a) the retiring allowance payable to a Prime Minister shall be at a rate of 3/5 of the highest annual salary paid to that Prime Minister;

(b) the retiring allowance payable to a legislator other than a Prime Minister shall be 1/2 the highest annual salary paid to that legislator.

(3) The retiring allowance paid to a legislator under this section shall be paid—

(a) with effect from the date on which the legislator becomes entitled to it pursuant to section 5 and shall continue to be paid during the legislator's lifetime; and

(b) monthly in arrears in equal instalments.

### 8. Application of section 7 to exceptional cases

(1) If a difficulty arises in connection with the application of section 7 to an exceptional case, Cabinet may make an order for the purpose of removing the difficulty and the order shall modify section 7 to the extent necessary to carry the order into effect.

(2) An order made under subsection (1) shall come into force on a day to be specified in the order.

(3) For the purposes of this section, an exceptional case includes a case where a legislator has in the past occupied a post as Minister but on the day of his or her retirement the legislator occupies a post as a member of the House of Assembly or Senate to which is attached a salary lower than that of Minister and the prejudicial effect shall be regarded as a difficulty.

(4) An order made under subsection (1) shall be subject to a negative resolution of the House of Assembly.

### 9. Gratuity and reduced allowance

(1) A legislator who is entitled to a retiring allowance under this Act may, at that legislator's option exercisable in accordance with subsection (2), be paid instead of retiring allowance at a rate provided for in section 7, an allowance at a rate of 3/4 of the retiring allowance provided for under section 8 together with a gratuity equal to 121/2 times the amount of the reduction made in the retiring allowance.

(2) The option referred to in subsection (1) shall be exercised by notice in writing to the appropriate Minister within 30 days of the date on which the legislator concerned ceases to be a member or within a further period as the appropriate Minister may allow.

### 10. Suspension of allowance

(1) A retiring allowance payable under this Act shall, if the legislator in receipt of the retiring allowance again becomes a legislator, be suspended during any period in respect of which the legislator is in receipt of a salary as a legislator.

(2) At the end of a period referred to in subsection (1), the rate of retiring allowance shall be recalculated in accordance with section 7.

(3) Where a retiring allowance is recalculated under subsection (2) and the legislator entitled to it opts to receive an allowance by way of a gratuity and reduced retiring allowance under section 9, then in calculating the amount of the gratuity payable, there shall be deducted the amount already paid to the legislator by way of other gratuities in respect of the retiring allowance payment suspended under subsection (1).

(4) A retiring allowance recalculated in accordance with subsection (2) shall be paid at the recalculated rate with effect from the date of cessation of the period of service as legislator which gave rise to the recalculation.

### 11. Awards not assignable

(1) An award or part of an award payable under this Act shall not be assignable or transferable except for the purpose of satisfying—

(a) a debt to the Crown;

(b) an order of a court for a payment of periodical sums of money towards the maintenance of a spouse, a former spouse or a child being a minor of a legislator to whom the award is payable.

(2) An award shall not be attached, sequestered, or levied upon, for or in respect of a debt unless

(a) by order of the Court; or

(b) the debt is due to the Crown.

### 12. Consolidated Fund

A payment made under this Act shall be a charge on and paid out of the Consolidated Fund and shall be paid by the Accountant General within a reasonable time of the due date.

### 13. Regulations

(1) The appropriate Minister may make regulations for the purpose of giving effect to the provisions of this Act.

(2) All regulations made under this section shall be subject to affirmative resolution.
