### AN ACT to provide for the registration of proprietary clubs.

Commencement [5 May 1945]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Registration of Proprietary Clubs Act.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act—

“**club**” means proprietary club;

“**intoxicating liquor**” means spirits, wine, beer, porter, cider, perry and any fermented, distilled or spirituous liquor which cannot, according to any Act in force relating to the sale of intoxicating liquor, be legally sold unless duly authorised by a licence granted under the Act;

“**proprietary club**” means any community or society who assembles or meets together in pursuit of a common object and whose property funds and assets do not belong jointly to the members thereof—and does not include any club registered as a company not for profit under the Companies Act;

“**secretary**” in relation to a club includes any officer of a club or other person performing the duties of a secretary and where there is no secretary, the proprietor of the club;

“**unregistered club**” means any club which requires to be registered under this Act, but which is not so registered or the registration of which has not been renewed or which has been struck off the register of clubs.

### 3. Obligation to register clubs

(1) The secretary of every club which occupies a house or part of a house which is habitually used for the purposes of a club and in which any intoxicating liquor is supplied to members or their guests, or any other premises which are habitually so used and in which any intoxicating liquor is so supplied, shall cause the club to be registered or its registration to be renewed as the case may be, in manner provided in this Act.

(2) The registration of a club under this Act shall not constitute the club premises licensed premises, nor authorise any sale of intoxicating liquor therein which would otherwise be illegal.

### 4. Keeping of register

(1) The Director of Finance and Planning shall keep a register of all registered clubs within Saint Lucia.

(2) The register shall contain the following particulars—

(a) the name and objects of the club;

(b) the address of the club;

(c) brief details of the terms of occupancy of the club premises;

(d) the name of the secretary and the name of the proprietor;

(e) the number of members;

(f) a printed copy of the rules of the club.

### 5. Mode of application for registration

(1) The secretary of every club shall not later than 15 January in every year, and in the case of a new club prior to the opening thereof and not later than 15 January in each succeeding year, apply to the Director of Finance and Planning to enter the club on the register or to renew its registration as the case may be.

(2) In support of such application, the secretary shall furnish a return to the Director of Finance and Planning signed by the secretary giving the particulars mentioned in section 4(2) in the case of a new club, and any changes in such particulars in the case of a renewal of registration.

(3) Such return shall also contain a statement signed by the secretary that there is kept upon the club premises a register of the names and addresses of the club members, and a record of the latest payment of their subscriptions, and a register of the names and addresses of visitors and guests.

### 6. Conditions for registration

(1) A club shall not be registered under this Act unless it complies with all the following conditions—

(a) that there should be a defined subscription payable by members of the club in advance;

(b) that correct accounts and books shall be kept showing the financial affairs of the club;

(c) that no intoxicating liquor shall be sold or supplied for consumption off the premises of the club;

(d) that intoxicating liquor shall only be supplied on the club premises at the request of members of the club and that a visitor shall not be supplied with intoxicating liquor unless on the invitation and in company of a member and that member shall, upon the admission of such visitor to the club premises, enter the name and address of the visitor and his or her own name in the visitors' book and the date of the visit;

(e) that a person shall not be allowed to become an honorary or temporary member of the club or be relieved of the payment of the regular entrance fee or subscription, except a person possessing certain qualifications defined in the rules or subject to conditions and regulations prescribed therein;

(f) that a person under the age of 16 years shall not be admitted a member of the club, unless the club is one primarily devoted to some athletic purpose, and in the latter case that no intoxicating liquor shall be sold or supplied to any member or person under 16 years of age;

(g) that it shall be kept open only between the hours of 7:00 a.m. and 12:00 p.m. daily except on Saturdays and days preceding public holidays when it may be kept open from 7:00 a.m. to 3:00 a.m. of the succeeding day.

(2) It is lawful for the Director of Finance and Planning, by written licence, on any special occasion, to extend the opening hours to such hours as he or she may specify on such licence.

A fee of $2.40 shall be charged for such licence.

### 7. Grant of application for registration

The Director of Finance and Planning, if he or she is satisfied—

(a) that the application of the secretary supplies the particulars mentioned in section 4(2);

(b) that provision is made in the rules of the club for the due observance of the conditions set out in section 6;

(c) that the locality and premises are suitable; and

(d) that the office bearers are fit and proper persons to manage a club,

shall grant an application to enter such club on the register or renew its registration as the case may be on payment of the prescribed fee.

### 8. Annual registration fee

An annual fee of $24 in respect of registration or renewal of registration shall be paid into the Treasury within 14 days of the notification by the Director of Finance and Planning of acceptance of an application made under section 5, and such registration or renewal of registration shall continue in force until 31 December following.

### 9. Striking club off register

(1) Where a club has been registered under this Act a district court on complaint in writing by any person may if it thinks fit make an order directing the club to be struck off the register on all or any of the following grounds namely—

(a) that the club has ceased to exist;

(b) that it is not conducted in good faith as a club or that it is kept or habitually used for an unlawful purpose;

(c) that there is frequent drunkenness on the club premises;

(d) that illegal sales of intoxicating liquor have taken place on the club premises;

(e) that any of the conditions laid down in section 6 are not being observed.

(2) If a magistrate grants a summons on the complaint, the summons shall be served on the secretary of the club or such other person as the magistrate may direct.

(3) Where a district court makes an order striking a club off the register it may, if it thinks fit by that order further direct that the premises occupied by the club shall not be used for the purposes of any club which requires to be registered under this Act for a specified period, which may extend, in case of a first order, to 12 months, or, in case of a second or subsequent order, to 5 years provided that any such direction may, for good cause shown, be subsequently cancelled or varied by the court.

### 10. Books and documents required to be kept on premises

Every registered club shall keep on the club premises—

(a) a register of ordinary members showing their addresses, and a record of the latest payment of their subscriptions;

(b) a separate register of temporary and honorary members showing the date of election and period of such membership;

(c) a copy of the rules of the club with any subsequent changes authenticated by the signature of the secretary;

(d) a record of all intoxicating liquor supplied to the club during the preceding 6 months.

### 11. Power to enter upon club premises

Any magistrate, the Director of Finance and Planning or the Commissioner of Police or any person authorised in writing by a magistrate, the Director of Finance and Planning or the Commissioner of Police may, at any time, during such hours as a registered club premises are open to members, enter upon the premises, call for and inspect all books and documents required to be kept on the premises and the accounts of the club and take copies of any entry in such books, documents or accounts and generally make inquiries to ascertain whether the club is being managed in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

### 12. Search Warrant for unregistered clubs

(1) If a magistrate is satisfied by information on oath that there is reasonable ground for supposing that intoxicating liquor is sold or kept for sale on premises of an unregistered club, he or she may grant a search warrant to any police officer.

(2) A search warrant granted under this section shall authorise such member to enter at any time any premises named therein within one month of the date thereof, if need be by force, and to inspect the premises and to seize any intoxicating liquor therein suspected to have been sold or kept for sale.

### 13. Offences and penalties

(1) If any intoxicating liquor is sold or kept for sale on the premises of an unregistered club the person selling or keeping for sale or authorising the selling or keeping for sale of such intoxicating liquor is liable to a fine not exceeding $240 or to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for a period not exceeding 2 months.

(2) Intoxicating liquor shall not be sold on a registered club's premises except to a member of such club and only for consumption on the premises and any person selling or obtaining any intoxicating liquor in contravention of the provisions of this subsection is liable to a fine not exceeding $120 or to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for a period not exceeding one month.

(3) A person who obstructs or prevents or attempts to obstruct any magistrate, the Director of Finance and Planning, the Commissioner of Police or a duly authorised person in the exercise of any of the powers by this Act conferred on him or her is liable to a fine not exceeding $120 or to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for a period not exceeding one month.

(4) If a secretary of a club knowingly makes a return which is false in any material particular he or she is liable to a penalty not exceeding $240 or to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for a period not exceeding 2 months.

### 14. Prosecution of offences

Any offence under this Act may be prosecuted summarily before a magistrate.
