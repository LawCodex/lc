### AN ACT respecting oaths and affirmations.

Commencement [20 December 1918]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Oaths and Affirmations Act.

#### Interpretation

### 2. Definition

In this Act “**officer**” means any person duly authorised to administer oaths.

#### Oaths

### 3. Power to administer oaths

(1) Every court, judge, or magistrate and every person having, by law or consent of parties, authority to hear and receive evidence, shall have power to administer or cause to be administered an oath to every witness who is legally called to give evidence before that court, judge or person.

(2) An officer of a court or authority who in fact administers an oath is, *prima facie*, deemed, for all purposes, to have been duly authorised to administer the oath.

### 4. General manner of administering oaths

(1) Any oath may be administered and taken in the form and manner following—

(a) the person taking the oath shall hold the New Testament, or, in the case of a Jew, the Old Testament, in his or her uplifted hand, and shall say or repeat after the officer administering the oath the words “I swear by Almighty God that ............................................” followed by the words of the oath;

(b) if a Roman Catholic or other person who so desires, instead of a Testament, he or she may hold a crucifix in his or her uplifted hand or keep his or her hand uplifted before a crucifix.

(2) The officer or other person administering the oath shall (unless the person about to take the oath voluntarily objects, or is physically incapable of so taking the oath) administer the oath in the form and manner aforesaid without question.

### 5. Oaths in Scotch form

(1) If any person to whom an oath is administered desires to swear with uplifted hand in the form and manner in which an oath is usually administered in Scotland, he or she shall be permitted so to do and the oath administered to him or her in such form and manner without further question.

(2) The person swearing shall swear standing and holding up his or her right hand and shall say or repeat the words of the oath after the officer administering the same.

(3) The oath shall commence “I swear by Almighty God, as I shall answer to God at the great day of judgment” and shall proceed with the words or the substance thereof prescribed by law omitting any further words of imprecation or calling to witness.

(4) A person sworn in this manner shall not be required to kiss any testament or other book or to take it into his or her hand.

### 6. Form person declares binding

An oath may be administered to a person in such form and with such ceremonies as he or she declares to be binding.

### 7. Oath in customary manner

Subject to the express provisions of this Act, an oath may be administered and taken in the manner and with the ceremonies heretofore customary.

### 8. Validity of oaths

Where an oath has been administered and taken, the fact that the person to whom the same was administered had, at the time of taking such oath, no religious belief, shall not for any purpose affect the validity of such oath, and every oath is deemed to be duly administered and taken.

#### Affirmations

### 9. Affirmation of witness

(1) If a person called or desiring to give evidence, objects on grounds of conscientious scruples to take an oath, or is objected to as incompetent to take an oath, such person may make the following affirmation—

“I solemnly affirm that the evidence to be given by me shall be the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth.”

(2) Upon the person making such solemn affirmation, his or her evidence shall be taken and have the same effect as if taken under oath.

### 10. Affirmation in other cases

If a person required or desiring to take an oath or to make an affidavit or deposition in a proceeding or on an occasion whereon or touching a matter respecting which an oath is required or is lawful, whether on taking office or otherwise, refuses or is unwilling to be sworn, on grounds of conscientious scruples, the court or judge, or other officer or person qualified to take affidavits or depositions, or before whom such person is to be sworn, shall permit such person, instead of being sworn to make his or her solemn affirmation in the words following, namely, “I, *A.B.* do solemnly affirm” etc., which solemn affirmation shall be of the same force and effect as if such person had taken an oath in the usual form.

### 11. Form of written affirmation

Every affirmation in writing shall commence “I ......................................... of ............................................. do solemnly and sincerely affirm,” and the form *in lieu* of *jurat* shall be “Affirmed at ...................................................., this ........... day of........................... Before me.”

### 12. Perjury

Any witness whose evidence is admitted or any person who makes an affirmation instead of taking an oath is liable to prosecution and punishment for perjury in all respects as if he or she had been sworn, and every affirmation is considered to have been duly administered.

#### Evidence of Children without Oath

### 13. When evidence of child of tender years receivable without oath

(1) In any legal proceeding where a child of tender years is offered as a witness, and such child does not, in the opinion of the judge, or other presiding officer, understand the nature of an oath, the evidence of such child may be received, though not given upon oath, if, in the opinion of the judge, justice or other presiding officer, as the case may be, such child is possessed of sufficient intelligence to justify the reception of the evidence, and understands the duty of speaking the truth.

(2) A case shall not be decided upon such evidence alone, and such evidence must be corroborated by some other material evidence.

(3) Any witness whose evidence is admitted under this section is liable on summary conviction, to prosecution and punishment for perjury in all respects as if he or she had been sworn.

#### Interpreters

### 14. Appointment of interpreter

The court, judge or magistrate or other person in authority may appoint and swear or cause to be sworn an interpreter whenever necessary to interpret the evidence of a witness, or any statement or document requiring interpretation.

### 15. Swearing of interpreter

Every interpreter, not being an officer of the court or an official interpreting as such in virtue of his or her office, shall be sworn in each cause or matter or proceeding to interpret truly.

#### Swearing of Jury

### 16. Manner prescribed by Criminal Code

The jury may be sworn in the manner prescribed in the Criminal Code.

#### Saving

### 17. Oaths, and proof of documents, otherwise

The provisions of this Act shall be in addition to, and not in derogation of, the Official Oaths Act or of any powers of proving documents given by any statute, or existing at law.
