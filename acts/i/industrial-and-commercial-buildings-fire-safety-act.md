### AN ACT to provide for the inspection and certification of industrial and commercial buildings in respect of the means of escape in case of fire, and other measures to protect the safety of persons employed therein or resorting thereto and for connected matters.

Commencement [7 November 1973]

### 1. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Industrial and Commercial Buildings (Fire Safety) Act.

### 2. Interpretation

(1) In this Act—

“**certificate**” includes an amended certificate;

“**Chief Fire Officer**” means the Chief Fire Officer appointed under section 3 of the Fire Service Act;

“**industrial and commercial buildings**” means any building or part thereof or a place in respect of which order may be made by the Minister under this Act;

“**inspector**” means a member of the Fire Service not below the rank of station officer appointed under section 3;

“**maintained**” means maintained in an efficient state in efficient working order, and in good repair;

“**Minister**” means the Minister charged with the responsibility of labour;

“**owner**” includes occupier;

“**register**” means the register required to be kept under section 18.

(2) For the purposes of this Act a person is employed in an industrial or commercial building where he or she is in that building having been contracted hired or engaged to perform work of any kind in that building irrespective of the length of time spent in that building in connection with such work and whether or not wages are paid.

(3) For the purposes of this Act a building is constructed, extended or converted for use as an industrial building before any date, if the construction, extension or conversion was begun before that date.

### 3. The Chief Fire Officer responsible for administration

(1) The Chief Fire Officer is responsible for the administration of this Act.

(2) The Chief Fire Officer may appoint the inspectors that he or she thinks necessary to assist him or her in the execution of this Act and assign to them their duties and regulate the manner in which they are to execute and perform the powers and duties of inspectors under this Act.

(3) Notice of the appointment of every inspector shall be published in the Gazette.

(4) The Chief Fire Officer shall furnish every inspector with a certificate of his or her appointment and an inspector when visiting a building to which any of the provisions of this Act applies shall if so required; produce the said certificate to the owner of the building or to any other person appearing ostensibly to be in charge.

### 4. Powers of Chief Fire Officer and Inspectors

(1) The Chief Fire Officer and every inspector shall for the purposes of the execution of this Act have power to do all or any of the following things, that is to say—

(a) to enter and examine at reasonable times any building and every part of it for the purpose of ascertaining the nature of the building, the availability of a water supply to the building, the means of ingress and egress from the building for the purpose of determining the means of danger in case of fire for persons employed there, and other material circumstances;

(b) to enter by day any building or part thereof and any place which he or she has reasonable cause to believe that explosive, highly inflammable or combustible materials are stored, kept or used;

(c) to take with him or her a police officer if he or she has any reasonable cause to apprehend any serious obstruction in the execution of his or her duty;

(d) to require the production of the register and certificate and to inspect, examine and copy any of them;

(e) to require any person in any building to give such information as it is in his or her power to give as to who is the owner or persons in charge of the building;

(f) to examine, either alone or in the presence of any other person as he or she thinks fit, with respect to matters under this Act any person in any building and to require every such person to be so examined and to sign a declaration of the truth of the matters respecting which he or she has been so examined of, so however that no one shall be required under this provision to answer any question or to give any evidence tending to incriminate himself or herself or in the case of a person who is married, his or her spouse.

(2) The owner of every building, his or her agents and servants shall furnish the means required by the Chief Officer or an inspector as necessary for an entry inspection, examination, inquiry or otherwise for the exercise of his or her owners under this Act in relation to that building.

(3) Any person who wilfully—

(a) delays an inspector in the exercise of any power under this section;

(b) fails to comply with the requisition of an inspector under this section;

(c) fails to produce any register, certificate or document which he or she is required by or under this Act to produce;

(d) wilfully withholds any information as to who is the owner or person in charge of any building; or

(e) conceals or prevents, attempts to conceal or prevent a person from appearing before or being examined by the Chief Fire Officer or inspector,

obstructs the Chief Fire Officer or an inspector in the execution of his or her duties under this Act.

### 5. Industrial and commercial building to be used only after certificate granted

An owner of an industrial or commercial building shall not use or permit the use of the building or any part of it unless there is in force in relation to that building a certificate that it is provided with such means of escape in case of fire for the persons employed there or resorting there as may reasonably be required in the circumstances of the case.

### 6. Application for certificate

An owner of an industrial or commercial building shall apply in the prescribed form to the Chief Fire Officer for a certificate in respect of the building.

### 7. The Chief Fire Officer to order inspection

The Chief Fire Officer shall cause an inspection to be made by an inspector in respect of every application made under section 6 and the inspector shall inspect such building and every part thereof and make a report in such form as the Chief Fire Officer may require.

### 8. Certificate of Chief Fire Officer

(1) Where the Chief Fire Officer after the inspection as provided by section 7 on being satisfied that an industrial or commercial building is provided with such means of escape as is mentioned in subsection 5 and any regulations made under this Act he or she shall grant a certificate.

(2) Where the Chief Fire Officer after the inspector as provided by section 7 is not satisfied that an industrial or commercial building is not provided with such means of escape as is mentioned in subsection 5 he or she shall by notice inform the applicant that he or she will not grant the certificate unless specified alterations are made. The notice shall specify the alterations and the time (or where further time has been allowed by the Chief Fire Officer) or such extended time within which such alterations are to be carried out.

(3) The owner shall within the time specified in the notice or such extended time carry out the alterations required by the notice and the Chief Fire Officer on being satisfied that the alterations have been carried out shall grant a certificate.

(4) Where the alterations are not carried out, a certificate is considered to have been refused at the expiration of the time specified in the notice or such further time as the Chief Fire Officer may have allowed.

### 9. Contents of certificate

(1) The certificate shall contain all the matters which were taken in account in granting the certificate and shall more particularly—

(a) specify precisely and in detail the means of escape provided;

(b) contain particulars as to the maximum number of persons to be employed in that building as a whole or where the circumstances warrant any specified part thereof;

(c) particulars as to any explosive, highly inflammable or combustible material stored, kept or used therein;

(2) The certificate shall be attached to the register.

### 10. Duty of occupier to maintain escape

It is the duty of every owner of an industrial or commercial building to cause all means of escape specified in the certificate to be properly maintained and kept free from obstruction.

### 11. Powers of Chief Fire Officer

(1) After a certificate has been granted it is lawful for the Chief Fire Officer to cause at any time any industrial and commercial building and every part of it to be examined for the purpose of ascertaining whether the existing means of escape are maintained or whether there is a change of conditions by reason of which the existing means of escape in case of fire are insufficient.

(2) If after a certificate has been granted, it is proposed to make any material extension or material structural alteration of an industrial or commercial building or any part of it or to increase the number of persons employed in that building or in any part specified in the certificate, or to begin to store or use explosive, highly inflammable or combustible material in that building or materially to increase the extent of such storage or use, the owner shall give notice in writing of the proposal to the Chief Fire Officer.

(3) If at any time the Chief Fire Officer is satisfied that by reason of the changed conditions the existing means of escape have become insufficient or if on receipt of the notice under subsection (2) he or she is of opinion that the conditions in regard to escape in case of fire will be affected, he or she may by notice in writing require the owner to make the alterations specified in the notice, within such time as may be specified in the notice and subject to the provisions of section 15 the owner shall carry out such alterations within such time as may be specified therein.

### 12. Dangerous condition

(1) If it appears to an inspector that dangerous conditions in regard to escape in case of fire exist in an industrial or commercial building he or she may give notice of this in writing to the Chief Fire Officer and it is the duty of the Chief Fire Officer to examine that building, and he or she may by notice in writing require the owner to make such alterations as specified in the notice within such period, as may be specified in the notice.

(2) Where notice is given to an owner under subsection (1) he or she shall subject to the provisions of section 15 within the time specified in the notice carry out the alterations required by the notice, and upon their being carried out the Chief Fire Officer shall amend the certificate or issue a new certificate and if the alterations are not carried out, the Chief Fire Officer shall without prejudice to the taking of proceedings cancel the certificate.

(3) Where notice is given by an inspector to the Chief Fire Officer under this section the Chief Fire Officer shall inform the inspector.

### 13. Proceedings in lieu of notice with respect dangerous escape

(1) If it appears to an inspector that the conditions in regard to escape in case of fire in any industrial or commercial building are so dangerous for that building or any part of it ought not to be used or ought not to be used for a particular process, work or purpose, until steps have been taken to remedy the danger, he or she may, *in lieu* of serving a notice on the Chief Fire Officer under section 12(1) make a complaint to a court of summary jurisdiction, and the court may, if satisfied of the matters aforesaid, by order prohibit the use of that building or part thereof, or its use for the particular process, work or purpose, until such works have been executed as are in the opinion of the court necessary to remedy the danger.

(2) Where any works have been executed under an order under subsection (1), the inspector shall give notice thereof to the Chief Fire Officer, who shall amend any certificate in force under section 9 in respect of that building, or issue other certificate, as the case may require.

### 14. Building not in conformity with regulations

Where a certificate has been issued in respect of an industrial or commercial building which is not in conformity with the regulations made under this Act the Chief Fire Officer shall serve a notice on the owner requiring him or her to make within a specified time such alterations as he or she considers necessary to bring that building into conformity with the regulations, and the provisions of section 11(3) and section 15 apply in relation to any such notice as they apply in relations to a notice of the Chief Fire Officer under the said section.

### 15. Right of appeal against refusal of certificate etc.

(1) If the occupier of an industrial or commercial building is aggrieved by—

(a) the refusal of the Chief Fire Officer to grant a certificate or amend a certificate;

(b) being required by the Chief Fire Officer under section 8, section 11(3) and section 14 or by an inspector under section 12 to carry out alteration to an industrial or commercial building;

(c) the period within which he or she is required to carry out such alterations;

(d) the cancellation of a certificate,

he or she may appeal by way of complaint within 21 days of the refusal, notice of requirement, or cancellation to a court of summary jurisdiction, and pending the final determination of the appeal no offence is committed under the above sections by reason that the premises to which the appeal relates are used or permitted to be used as an industrial or commercial building without a certificate.

(2) The decision of the Court is final and conclusive.

### 16. Removal of explosive highly inflamable or combustible material

Where as a result of an entry and inspection under section 4 the Chief Fire Officer or an inspector finds explosive highly inflammable or combustible material stored, collected, deposited or used in any building or place and believes that any such materials are likely to cause a fire hazard the Chief Fire Officer or an inspector shall serve a notice on the owner or occupier of the building or place specifying the nature and location of such materials and requesting their removal within such period as specified in the notice.

### 17. General safety provisions in case of fire

(1) While any person is within an industrial or commercial building for the purpose of employment or meals the stairs of that building or any room therein in which the person is, and any doors which afford a means of exit for persons employed in that building from any other room or from any other enclosure in that building in which any work or process is carried on, shall not be locked or fastened in such manner that they cannot be easily and immediately opened from the inside.

(2) A door opening on to any staircase or corridor from a room in which more than 5 persons are employed, and in the case of an industrial or commercial building constructed or converted after the coming into operation of this section all other doors affording a means of escape from any room, enclosure or area where any process or work is carried on at any time shall except in the case of sliding doors, be constructed to open outwards.

(3) Subject to the provisions of this Act where any building has been constructed or converted before the coming into operation of this Act for use as an industrial or commercial building where more than 5 persons are employed in the same building above the ground floor, any door which is not kept continuously open, at the foot of a staircase affording a means of exit from that building shall, except in the case of sliding doors, be constructed to open outwards.

(4) Every hoist or liftway inside an industrial or commercial building constructed after the coming into operation of this section shall, subject as hereinafter provided, be completely enclosed with fire resisting materials, and all means of access to the hoist or lift shall be fitted with doors of fire-resisting materials; however, any such hoisting or lift way shall be enclosed at the top only by some material easily broken by fire, or be provided with a vent at the top.

(5) Every window, door or exit affording means of escape in case of fire or giving access thereto other than the means of exit in ordinary case, shall be distinctly and conspicuously marked by a notice printed in red letters of an adequate size and shall cause the same notice to be maintained.

(6) An employer shall cause the contents of any room in an industrial and commercial building in which persons are employed or to which persons have resort to be so arranged or disposed that there is a free passage way for all persons employed in or resorting to that room to a means of escape in case of fire.

(7) In every industrial and commercial building effective means capable of being operated without exposing any person to undue risk shall be provided and maintained for giving warning in case of fire, which shall be clearly available throughout that building.

(8) In an industrial or commercial building where more than 5 persons are employed in the same building above the ground floor or where explosive, or highly inflammable or combustible materials are stored, or to which persons are resorting effective steps shall be taken to ensure that all the persons employed are familiar with the means of escape in case of fire and their use and the routine to be followed in case of fire and that persons resorting thereto may readily be able to discover the means of escape.

### 18. The Register

(1) An owner shall cause to be kept in an industrial or commercial building in respect of which a certificate has been granted a register in the prescribed form wherein particulars as prescribed shall be entered immediately after the grant of a certificate and likewise upon a change of any of the particulars as prescribed in the certificate.

(2) The owner shall cause the register to be preserved and available for inspection by the Chief Fire Officer or any inspector.

### 19. Offences

(1) Any person who obstructs the Chief Fire Officer or an inspector in the execution of his or her powers or duties under this Act is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $100 or to imprisonment for a term of 2 months.

(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act an owner who uses or permits an industrial or commercial building to be used in contravention of section 5 is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $500 or to imprisonment for a term of 6 months and if the contravention is continued after conviction, he or she subject to section 20 commits a further offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $50 for each day in which the contravention is so continued.

(3) A person who—

(a) forges or counterfeits a certificate required under this Act;

(b) gives or signs such certificate knowing it to be false in any material particular;

(c) knowingly utters or makes use of such certificates so forged, counterfeited or falsified as aforesaid;

(d) falsely pretends to be an inspector;

(e) wilfully connives at any such forging, counterfeiting, giving or signing, altering, making use or pretending as aforesaid;

(f) wilfully makes a false entry in a notice, the certificate of the register required by order or for the purposes of this Act;

(g) wilfully makes or signs a false declaration required by or under or for the purposes of this Act,

is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $500 or to imprisonment for a term of 6 months.

(4) Subject to the provisions of this Act or under the regulations made under it an owner who fails to perform or cause any duty to be performed as provided by this Act or by the regulations in relation to or in connection with in industrial or commercial building is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $250 or to imprisonment for a term of 3 months and if the contravention in respect of which he or she was convicted is continued after the conviction he or she subject to the provisions of section 20 commits a further offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $25 for each day on which the contravention is so continued; however, an owner shall not be convicted of an offence under this section where the act, omission or default resulting in the failure or neglect of duty was not caused by his or her act omission or default.

(5) An owner or occupier who neglects or refuses to remove any article in respect of which a notice has been served on him or her under section 16 is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $100 or to imprisonment of a term of one month and if the contravention in respect of which he or she was convicted is continued after the conviction he or she subject to the provisions of section 20 commits a further offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $10 for each day on which the contravention is so continued.

### 20. Power of Court to order cause of contravention to be remedied

Where an occupier of any building is convicted of an offence under this Act, the court may, in addition to or instead of imposing a fine, order him or her, within the time specified in the order, to take such steps as may be so specified for remedying the matters in respect of which the contravention occurred, and may on application enlarge the time so specified.

Where such an order is made the occupier shall not be liable under this Act in respect of the continuation of the contravention during the time allowed by the Court, but if, after the expiration of that time as originally specified or enlarged by subsequent order, the order is not complied with the occupier is liable to a fine not exceeding $50 for each day on which the non compliance continues.

### 21. Regulations

(1) The Minister may make regulations with regard to carrying out or giving effect to the provisions of this Act and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing, the regulations may be made in particular with respect to all or any of the following, namely—

(a) the means of escape from any industrial and commercial building;

(b) as to the collecting, storing, depositing, keeping or using of explosive, highly inflammable or combustible materials in any building;

(c) as to the provision and maintenance of a fire warning system in an industrial or commercial building;

(d) as to the provisions of such fire safety appliances in an industrial and commercial building;

(e) as to the testing and examination of such warnings the appliances as provided under paragraph (c) and (d);

(f) for prescribing anything which by this Act to be or may be prescribed.

(2) Any regulations made under this section is subject to negative resolution of the House of Assembly.

### 22. Service of documents

(1) A document including a summons, or order required or authorised to be served under this Act may be served—

(a) on any person by delivering it to him or her, or by leaving it at or sending it by registered post to his or her residence;

(b) on any firm by delivering it to any partner of the firm, or by leaving it at, or sending it by registered post to the office of the firm;

(c) on the owner of the building (including where such owner is a corporate body or association of persons whether incorporated or not), by delivering it to the secretary or clerk at the office of that body or association or sending it by registered post to such clerk or secretary at such office.

(2) Any such document may be addressed for the purpose of the service thereof on the occupier of any building to “the owner” at the proper postal address of the building, without further name or description.

(3) The foregoing provisions of this section shall apply with the necessary modifications to documents required to be sent to any person, firm, or owner, and to the sending addressing and delivering of such documents.

### 23. Proceedings

The Chief Fire Officer and any inspector may prosecute, conduct or defend proceedings arising under this Act or the regulations made thereunder in the discharge of their duties as Chief Fire Officer or inspector as the case may be under this Act.

### 24. Evidence

Where an entry is required by this Act or any regulations made thereunder to be made in the register, the entry made by the owner of an industrial or commercial building or on his or her behalf shall, as against him or her be admissible as evidence of the facts stated in it and the fact that any entry so required with respect to the observance of any provision of this Act or any regulation made thereunder has not been made is admissible as evidence that provision has not been observed.

### 25. Construction

Despite the provisions of any enactment—

(a) relating to the approval of plans, the erection, re-erection, removal, repair of any building;

(b) regulating the licensing and the carrying of any business, concern or trade; or

(c) relating to and regarding the storage depositing, keeping, sale or disposal of any article, matter or thing,

the provisions of this Act where they are applicable shall be construed and made applicable to any building in respect of which such provisions apply and shall not be construed as being in conflict with any of the provisions of the enactment.
