### An ACT to regulate the manufacture of sugar in Saint Lucia, the disposal of such sugar, the allocation to local manufacturers of quotas for exportation and for sale for local consumption and for other connected purposes.

Commencement [28 May 1938]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Sugar Control Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

“**quota**” means the total quantity of sugar which may be manufactured, held in stock, exported, disposed of locally or otherwise in accordance with an order made by the Governor General under this Act;

“**quota period**” means any period in respect of which any quota has been fixed by an Order of the Governor General under this Act;

“**stocks**” means sugar in any factory or warehouse or in transit but does not include foreign sugar in bond or sugar stored in any warehouse or other place for the purpose of distribution for consumption within Saint Lucia;

“**sugar**” includes fancy molasses and sugar in any of its commercial forms except the product sold as final molasses.

### 3. Powers of Governor General

(1) The Governor General may by order—

(a) fix the quota which may be—

(i) manufactured,

(ii) exported,

(iii) sold or delivered for consumption within Saint Lucia, or

(iv) held in stock for disposal in addition to (ii) and (iii) during any quota period specified in any such order, and prescribe the conditions under which it shall be granted and under which any such quota shall be disposed of;

(b) allocate the quota fixed amongst the manufacturers for each factory and at any time vary and adjust such allocation;

(c) prescribe the conditions as to the manner of export of any sugar;

(d) regulate the sale of and price at which any person shall sell or cause to be sold sugar manufactured in Saint Lucia and intended for local consumption either by wholesale or retail;

(e) define the meaning of “wholesale” and “retail” as applied to the sale or offer for sale of sugar manufactured in Saint Lucia and required or intended for local consumption;

(f) regulate or provide for such other matters concerning the manufacture, export, sale and disposal of sugar as may be necessary for the carrying out of the purposes and provisions of this Act.

(2) If there shall be any contravention of any of the provisions of an order made under the preceding subsection the owner and the manufacturer of the sugar, the subject matter of the contravention, and every other person who is party to the contravention is liable on summary conviction for each offence to a penalty not exceeding $96 and in addition to a further penalty not exceeding $2.40 in respect of each hundred weight of sugar manufactured, exported, sold, delivered or otherwise disposed of, or in respect of each hundred weight of sugar in respect of which there has been failure to manufacture, export, sell, deliver, hold or otherwise dispose of, in contravention of the order.

However, such owner such manufacturer or such other person who is party to the said contravention, is not liable to conviction under the provisions of this section if he or she proves to the satisfaction of the magistrate that the contravention thereof was neither wilful nor due to negligence.

### 4. Power of entry and seizure

(1) The Governor General may authorise any person with or without assistance to enter any factory, premises or land in or on which any sugar is stored or kept to ascertain whether provisions of any order made under subsection of section 3(1) have been complied with and if any such provisions have not been complied with, then to seize and remove or detain any sugar found therein in contravention of any such provisions.

(2) The Governor General may by order declare in what manner any sugar seized as aforesaid shall be disposed of and may authorise any person to supervise such disposal.

(3) The owner or manufacturer shall not be entitled to claim compensation for any sugar destroyed or otherwise disposed of in accordance with such order.

### 5. The Governor General may require returns relating to sugar

(1) The Governor General may require any person by order served on him or her to make and submit to him or her, within such time as may be specified in the order, a return in the form of a Declaration giving full and accurate particulars of all or any of the following matters, namely—

(a) the sugar manufactured by him or her during any period specified in an order;

(b) the stocks of sugar belonging to him or her or in his or her possession power or control on any particular date or during any period specified in an order, the place of storage of the same, and if such sugar was acquired by him or her, the person from whom he or she acquired the same;

(c) the sugar disposed of by him or her, either by sale or delivery locally or by exportation during any period specified in an order;

(d) an estimate of his or her total sugar production and of the quantity intended to be exported during any period;

(e) any other particulars required for the purposes of this Act.

(2) Any person who fails or neglects to comply with an order under the preceding subsection is liable on summary conviction to a penalty not exceeding $96.

### 6. Jurisdiction

Proceedings under this Act may be instituted before a magistrate at any time within one year from the time when the cause of complaint arose; and the procedure shall be the same as the procedure in respect of offences punishable on summary conviction.

However, such proceedings shall not be instituted without the consent in writing of the Attorney General.
