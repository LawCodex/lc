### AN ACT to provide for the establishment of Constituency Councils and for related matters.

Commencement [30 April 2012]

## PART 1 PRELIMINARY

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Constituency Councils Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**“constituency”** has the same meaning as “electoral district” in section 2 of the Elections Act;

**“Constituency Council”** or **“Council”** means a body of local representatives who have been appointed in accordance with section 4 (Inserted by Cap. 17.19);

**“member”** means member of a Constituency Council;

**“Ministry”** means the Ministry responsible for local government;

**“Minister”** means the Minister responsible for local government;

**“repealed Acts”** means the Local Authorities Ordinance, the Castries Corporation Act and the Castries Corporation (Termination of Council) (Repeal) Act.

### 3. Purpose

The purpose of this Act is—

(a) to improve the delivery of social services to constituencies; and

(b) to assist in the management of resources to be used for the development of constituencies,

through the establishment of Constituency Councils.

## PART 2 ESTABLISHMENT, CONSTITUTION AND PROCEDURE OF CONSTITUENCY COUNCILS

### 4. Establishment of Councils

(1) Subject to section 2, there are established 15 Councils for the constituencies specified in the Schedule.

(2) For the purposes of this Act, the Castries Constituency Council is the Council for the following constituencies—

(a) Castries North;

(b) Castries Central;

(c) Castries East;

(d) Castries South.

(3) For the purposes of this Act the constituency of Anse La Raye-Canaries shall have the following two Councils—

(a) The Anse La Raye Council.

(b) The Canaries Council.

### 5. Constitution of Councils

(1) Each Council shall consist of not less than nine and not more than fifteen members appointed by the Minister by Order published in the Gazette*.*

(2) Members appointed under subsection (1) shall be selected from the following categories—

(a) a national civil society group;

(b) a civil society group or sector within the constituency;

(c) a nominee from each of the political parties represented in the House of Assembly;

(d) not less than three members appointed from among persons of proven capacity in matters relating to social development, agriculture, community development, tourism, environmental matters, health and business or any other areas relevant to the development of the community;

(e) a representative of the Ministry of local government who is a public servant.

(3) The Minister shall appoint a Chairperson and Deputy Chairperson from the members of the following Councils—

(i) Babonneau,

(ii) Castries South East,

(iii) Dennery North,

(iv) Dennery South,

(v) Micoud North,

(vi) Micoud South,

(vii) Vieux Fort North,

(viii) Laborie-Augier,

(ix) Choiseul-Saltibus,

(x) Anse La Raye, and

(xi) Canaries.

(4) The Minister shall appoint a Mayor and Deputy Mayor from the members of the following Councils—

(i) Gros Islet,

(ii) Vieux Fort South,

(iii) Soufriere, and

(iv) Castries.

(5) The duties of the Secretary of each Council shall be performed by the person assigned by the members for that purpose.

### 6. Functions of Councils

The functions of the Councils are—

(a) to assist with the delivery of services to constituents;

(b) to make recommendations to the Minister on programmes and projects which will benefit constituencies;

(c) to carry out activities designated by the Ministry on behalf of the residents of a constituency;

(d) to identify and prioritize the needs of constituencies based on supporting data;

(e) to serve as the liaison between constituents and the Ministry;

(f) to refer constituents requiring services in a constituency to the most appropriate Government agency that provides the services required;

(g) to build relevant databases with respect to a constituency in collaboration with other government departments; and

(h) to raise revenue in a manner provided by Parliament to finance the delivery of services to constituents.

### 7. Tenure

(1) A member shall hold office for a period of 2 years unless the member dies, resigns or has his appointment revoked.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), a person who is appointed to fill a vacancy created by the death, resignation or removal from office of a former member, shall hold office only for the unexpired portion of the term of that former member.

(3) A member is eligible for reappointment for a further term on the expiration of the term of his appointment.

### 8. Honorarium

Members are eligible for such honorarium for their attendance at meetings as the Minister, with the approval of the Cabinet determines.

### 9. Resignation

(1) The Chairperson or Mayor of a Council may resign from office by submitting a written notice of resignation to the Minister and ceases to be the Chairperson or Mayor and a member from the date the Minster receives the notice, unless another date of resignation is provided for in the notice.

(2) A member other than the Chairperson or Mayor may resign from office by submitting a written notice of resignation to the Minister through the Chairperson or Mayor of a Council and ceases to be a member from the date the Minister receives the notice.

### 10. Revocation of appointment

(1) The Minister shall revoke the appointment of a member if the member—

(a) without reasonable excuse, fails to attend three consecutive meetings of the Council;

(b) is declared a bankrupt under Chapter 9 of the Commercial Code of the Revised Laws of Saint Lucia 1957;

(c) is declared by a court to be incapacitated by physical or mental illness;

(d) engages in fraudulent or other dishonest behaviour or behaviour conflicting with the discharge of his or her duties as a member of the Council;

(e) has been charged and convicted of a criminal offence except where the offence is a minor traffic offence or is spent in accordance with the Criminal Records (Rehabilitation of Offenders) Act.

(2) The Minister may for reasonable cause revoke the appointment of a Council before the expiration of the tenure of the Council.

### 11. Leave of absence

The Minister may, in writing, grant leave of absence to a member and may appoint a person to act temporarily in the place of that member.

### 12. Councils to regulate own procedure

Except as provided in this Act and Regulations made under this Act, each Council shall regulate its own procedure.

### 13. Meetings

Each Council shall meet at such times as are necessary for the transaction of business.

### 14. Presiding at meetings

(1) The Chairperson or Mayor of a Council shall preside at meetings of a Council.

(2) If the Chairperson or Mayor is absent from a meeting but the Deputy Chairperson or Deputy Mayor is present, the Deputy Chairperson or Deputy Mayor shall preside.

(3) If the Chairperson and Deputy Chairperson or the Mayor and Deputy Mayor, as the case may be, are absent from a meeting of a Council, the members shall select a member present at the meeting to preside.

### 15. Quorum

A majority of the members holding office present at the time a meeting is held constitutes the quorum necessary for the transaction of business.

### 16. Decisions

(1) Decisions of each Council are to be made by a majority of votes of members present and voting at the meeting.

(2) In the event of an equality of votes, the Chairperson or Mayor has a casting vote.

### 17. Minutes of meetings

(1) Each Council shall cause to be kept, minutes of its meetings and of meetings of any committee established by it.

(2) The minutes of a Council meeting shall be made available within 2 weeks of a request by a constituent whether in electronic form or otherwise.

### 18. Members of Parliament entitled to receive notice of meetings

(1) The Secretary to each Council shall notify the Member of the House of Assembly representing the particular constituency or constituencies in the case of the Castries Constituency Council.

(2) A Member of the House of Assembly who is notified of a meeting may, if he or she wishes to attend the meeting.

### 19. Experts

(1) A Council may, with the approval of the Minister, engage persons having special or technical knowledge to assist in carrying out its functions.

(2) An expert engaged under subsection (1)—

(a) may, on the invitation of the Chairperson or Mayor of a Council, attend meetings of a Council, but does not have the right to vote;

(b) shall receive such remuneration as the Minister of Finance determines.

### 20. Documents

Documents made by a Council and decisions of a Council must be signified under the hand of the Chairperson or Mayor of that Council, or any member authorised to act in that behalf.

### 21. Public meetings

(1) Each Council shall hold biannual public meetings at a time approved by the Minister to apprise constituents of the progress and agenda of the Council.

(2) A recommendation made at a public meeting conducted under subsection (1) shall be forwarded to the Minister.

(3) A Member of the House of Assembly for a Constituency must be given at least 7 days written notice of a public meeting and may, if he or she so wishes make an address at a public meeting.

### 22. Public hearings

A Council may hold public hearings on matters which are of interest to the constituency or constituencies it represents.

### 23. Committees of Councils

(1) A Council may appoint committees to assist in the performance of its functions under this Act.

(2) A Council may delegate any of its functions to a committee.

(3) The Chairperson or the Mayor of a Council shall be an *ex officio* member of a committee of that Council.

(4) A committee is to comprise not more than five persons.

### 24. Finances

The expenses of a Council are to be defrayed out of—

(a) such amounts as may be allocated annually for special purposes by Parliament for the use and operations of the Council;

(b) money received by way of gifts, grants or donations;

(c) money becoming payable to or vested in the Council;

(d) money raised by the Council in the manner prescribed by Parliament;

(e) money allocated by the Minister responsible for Finance for a specific purpose, program or project.

### 25. Application of funds

The funds of a Council shall be applied towards—

(a) the operating expenses of the Council;

(b) the payment of the Council's programmes, activities and projects; and

(c) other expenses as authorized by the Minister.

### 26. Financial year

The financial year of a Council begins on 1 April and ends on 31 March in each year.

### 27. Accounts and Audit

(1) Each Council must keep proper records of its accounts in relation to the execution of its functions under this Act for a period of not less than 7 years.

(2) Each Council shall prepare a statement of accounts in respect of each financial year.

(3) The Director of Audit may at any time and shall, on the direction of the Minister responsible for Finance, carry out an investigation or audit of the accounts of each Council.

(4) The Minister responsible for Finance may appoint a special auditor to audit the accounts of a Council on any project or activity undertaken by that Council.

(5) Each Council shall, as soon as practicable after the end of each financial year, and not later than 6 months after the end of that financial year, submit to the Minister a report containing in respect of each Council—

(a) a detailed account of its activities during the preceding financial year, in such detail as the Minister directs; and

(b) a statement of the audited accounts for that financial year.

(6) A copy of the report and statement of audited accounts referred to in subsection (5) shall be laid before Parliament and published in the Gazette not later than 3 months from the date of receipt by the Minister.

### 28. Directions

The Minister shall give the Councils directions in relation to a matter relevant to the performance of its functions under this Act.

### 29. Declaration of interest and abstention from voting

(1) A member who has an interest in a matter before a Council shall declare the nature of the interest at the first meeting of the Council at which it is practicable to do so and shall leave the meeting when the matter comes up for discussion.

(2) A declaration and the departure of a member from a meeting in accordance with subsection (1) must be noted in the minutes of the meeting.

(3) A member shall not—

(a) vote in respect of a matter before a Council in which that member has an interest; or

(b) seek to influence the vote of any other member of a Council in relation to the matter.

(4) A member who fails to comply with subsection (3) commits an offence and upon summary conviction, is liable to a fine not exceeding $10,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding one year or to both.

(5) Notwithstanding subsection (4), where a member fails to comply with subsection (3), the failure to comply amounts to misconduct in which case section 10 applies.

## PART 3 MISCELLANEOUS

### 30. Amendment of Schedule

(1) The Minister may, by Order, amend the Schedule.

(2) An Order made under subsection (1) is subject to negative resolution.

### 31. Regulations

(1) The Minister may make regulations for regulating the functions of the Mayor, Deputy Mayor, Chairperson and Deputy Chairperson and for otherwise giving effect to any of the provisions of the Act.

(2) Regulations made under subsection (1) are subject to negative resolution.

### 32. Transitional

Upon the commencement of this Act—

(a) all land and other property of every kind, vested immediately before the commencement of this Act in Councils appointed under the repealed Acts shall vest in the Councils appointed under this Act;

(b) all rights, privileges and all liabilities and other obligations which Councils appointed under the repealed Act were entitled to before the commencement of this Act, are transferred and conferred or imposed upon the Councils appointed under this Act.

### 33. Savings

(1) This Act does not affect the rights or claims of any person appointed under the repealed Acts or the pension or gratuity of any person who, prior to the coming into operation of this Act, would have been entitled to such a pension or gratuity and such a person shall be deemed to have been appointed under this Act and all such rights and claims to such pension or gratuity shall be preserved and settled in accordance with the provisions of the Pensions Act and any other relevant law.

(2) A person who immediately before the coming into operation of this Act, was an employee of a Council under a repealed Act continues to be an employee of a Council, unless otherwise determined by that Council.

### 34. Repeal

The following enactments are repealed—

(a) Local Authorities Ordinance, Cap. 242 of the Revised Laws of Saint Lucia 1957;

(b) Castries Corporation Act, No. 22 of 1967;

(c) Castries Corporation (Termination of Council) (Repeal) Act, No. 4 of 1982.
