### AN ACT to authorise the conditional discharge of prisoners under licences.

Commencement [2 July 1884]

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Convicts Licence Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

“**imprisonment**” means imprisonment in the prison, with or without hard labour, as the case may be, and includes penal servitude;

“**licence-holder**” means any person to whom a licence is granted under this Act, and who holds or is entitled to hold such licence;

“**prison**” means the Bordelais Correctional Facility;

“**prisoner**” means any person undergoing or hereafter to undergo imprisonment in Saint Lucia, whether under the sentence of any court of Saint Lucia or any other country, the term of whose original sentence was or shall be not less than 2 years.

*Granting of Licence*

### 3. Governor General may grant, revoke or alter licence

(1) The Governor General may, by order under his or her hand, grant to any prisoner in Saint Lucia a licence to be at large during such portion of his or her imprisonment within Saint Lucia as to the Governor General may seem fit; and the Governor General may, by order under his or her hand, revoke or alter such licence at his or her pleasure.

(2) Such licence may be in the Form contained in Schedule 1, or in such other form as the Governor General may prescribe.

### 4. Copies of licence

On any licence being granted, a copy thereof shall be kept in the Government Office, and another copy thereof shall be sent to the Commissioner of Police, and shall be kept by him or her at his or her office.

*Privileges and Liabilities of Licence-holder*

### 5. Rights of Licence-holder

(1) So long as a licence continues in force and un-revoked, the licence-holder is not liable to be imprisoned by reason of his or her sentence, but shall be allowed to go and remain at large according to the terms of such licence.

(2) A licence-holder may, despite his or her conviction for felony, acquire and hold personal property, and may maintain actions for its recovery or in respect of any damage thereto, if and during such time as such licence continues un-revoked and un-forfeited.

### 6. Conditions imposed upon Licence-holder

(1) Every licence-holder shall—

(a) within 24 hours after he or she is liberated from prison, notify the place of his or her residence to the magistrate of the district in which he or she resides, and whenever he or she changes such residence within the same district, he or she shall notify such change of residence to the magistrate of such district, and whenever he or she changes his or her residence from the district of one magistrate into the district of another magistrate, he or she shall notify such change of residence to the magistrate of the district which he or she is leaving and also to the magistrate of the district into which he or she goes to reside; and

(b) once in every month, report himself or herself and the place of his or her residence either to the Commissioner of Police or to the police officer in charge of the nearest police station, and such report shall be made at such time in the month, and either personally or by letter, as the Commissioner of Police may direct.

(2) It is the duty of every police officer who receives any such report to forward the same immediately to the Commissioner of Police.

*Forfeiture and Revocation of Licence*

### 7. Forfeiture of licence on conviction

If any licence-holder is convicted, either by the verdict of a jury or upon his or her own confession, of any offence for which he or she is indicted, his or her licence shall be forfeited by virtue of such conviction.

### 8. Breach of certain conditions of licence, and punishment

If any licence-holder fails to comply with any of the requirements of section 6, he or she in every such case, unless he or she proves, to the satisfaction of the court, before which he or she is tried, that he or she did his or her best to act in conformity with the law, commits an offence under this Act, and on his or her being convicted thereof, his or her licence may, in the discretion of the court, be forfeited or, if the original term of imprisonment in respect of which his or her licence was granted has expired at the date of his or her conviction, it is lawful for the court to sentence him or her to imprisonment, with, or without hard labour, for any term not exceeding one year, or if the said original term has not expired, but the unexpired remainder thereof is a lesser period than one year then, in addition to the forfeiture of his or her licence, to sentence him or her to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, to commence at the expiration of the said original term of imprisonment, for such a time as together with the unexpired remainder of his or her said original term of imprisonment, will not exceed one year.

### 9. Breach of other conditions, and punishment

If any licence-holder—

(a) fails to produce his or her licence when required so to do by any judge or magistrate before whom he or she is brought charged with any offence, or by any police or other constable in whose custody he or she is, and also fails to make any reasonable excuse for not producing the same; or

(b) breaks any of the other conditions of his or her licence by an act that is not of itself punishable either on indictment or on summary conviction,

he or she commits an offence, and on conviction, is liable to imprisonment, with or without hard labour, for any term not exceeding 3 months.

### 10. Power to arrest and detain Licence-holder in certain cases

Any police or other constable may, without warrant, take into custody any licence-holder whom he or she reasonably suspects of having committed any offence, or of having broken any of the conditions of his or her licence, and may detain him or her in custody until he or she can be taken before a magistrate and dealt with according to law.

### 11. Forwarding certificate to Governor general after conviction

When any licence-holder is convicted of an offence punishable on summary conviction under this or any other Act, the magistrate convicting the prisoner shall without delay forward, by post or otherwise, to the Governor General a certificate in the Form contained in Schedule 2, and thereupon the licence of the said licence-holder may be revoked in manner provided by this Act.

### 12. Proceedings where licence is revoked by the Governor general

(1) Whenever the Governor General revokes a licence and signifies such revocation to any magistrate, such magistrate shall, if the licence-holder is not already in custody, immediately issue his or her warrant for his or her apprehension.

(2) On any licence-holder being brought before a magistrate, either on such warrant or otherwise, after the Governor General's revocation signified as aforesaid, the magistrate shall, by warrant under his or her hand, commit such licence-holder to prison, and thereupon the licence-holder shall be remitted to his original sentence, and shall undergo the residue thereof as if no licence had been granted.

### 13. Consequences of forfeiture of licence after conviction

Where a licence is forfeited by a conviction for any offence, the licence-holder shall, after undergoing the punishment for the offence in consequence of which his or her licence has been forfeited, further undergo a term of imprisonment equal to the portion of his or her term of imprisonment which remained unexpired at the time when his or her licence was granted, and he or she shall for such purpose be committed to prison under the order of the court or magistrate before which or whom he or she is convicted there to be dealt with accordingly.

*Miscellaneous*

### 14. Prosecution of offences

Any offence under this Act may be prosecuted summarily before a magistrate.

### 15. Forms

The Governor General may make and publish forms to be used under this Act.

### 16. Saving of Royal Prerogative

This Act shall not affect Her Majesty's Royal prerogative of Mercy, or any like prerogative or power vested in the Governor General.
