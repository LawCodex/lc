### AN ACT to provide for the records of civil status and for related matters.

Commencement [17 March 2010]

## PART 1 PRELIMINARY

### 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Civil Status Act.

### 2. Interpretation

In this Act—

**“Adjudicator”** means an Adjudicator appointed under section 25;

**“Assistant Registrar”** means the Assistant Registrar of Civil Status appointed under section 4;

“**Birth Certificate**” means a record of birth that is issued by the State and is the only officially recognised birth record of a person; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

**“Civil Status Assistants”** means the Civil Status Assistants appointed under section 4;

**“Civil Status Officer”** means—

(a) the Registrar of Civil Status;

(b) a District Registrar;

(c) a Marriage Officer; or

(d) a status officer as defined under the Civil Code;

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

“**date**” where the context permits, means a day of the month as specified by a number, a month and a year; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

“**Deed Poll**” means a change of name by notarial instrument that is registered at the office of the Registrar and the Registry of Deeds and Mortgages; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

“**District Registrar**” means a District Registrar appointed under section 5;

“**faith-based organisation**” includes a church or other religious denomination or institution that is registered with the Ministry responsible for registering a church or other religious denomination or institution; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

“**Marriage Officer**” means a Marriage Officer appointed under section 17(1)(a)(iii);

“**Minister**” means the Minister responsible for Justice;

“**record of baptism or dedication**” means a record of the baptism or dedication of a person from a faith-based organisation and includes a 'baptism and birth certificate'; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

“**Registrar**” means the Registrar of Civil Status appointed under section 4.

## PART 2 ADMINISTRATION

### 3. Records of civil status and registration districts

(1) Records of civil status are —

(a) records of births;

(b) records of adoptions;

(c) records of marriages;

(d) records of deaths;

(e) records of burials; and

(f) records of baptisms or dedications.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(2) For the purpose of registering acts of civil status, Saint Lucia is divided into eleven districts as follows—

District 1 – Castries

District 2 – Anse La Raye

District 3 – Soufriere

District 4 – Choiseul

District 5 – Laborie

District 6 – Vieux Fort

District 7 – Micoud

District 8 – Dennery

District 9 – Gros Islet

District 10 – Babonneau

District 11 – Canaries.

(3) Cabinet may, by Order published in the Gazette, amend subsection (2) to add or delete any district as Cabinet thinks fit.

### 4. Registrar, Assistant Registrars, Civil Status Assistants

(1) There shall be —

(a) a Registrar of Civil Status;

(b) Deputy Registrars of Civil Status; and

(c) Assistant Registrars of Civil Status;

appointed by the Judicial and Legal Services Commission.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(2) There shall be one or more Civil Status Assistants appointed by the Public Service Commission.

(3) The Minister may by Regulations prescribe the qualifications of Civil Status Assistants.

(4) The Registrar shall be the chief civil status officer and shall be responsible for—

(a) drawing up and altering records of civil status;

(b) keeping and maintaining records of civil status;

(c) the custody of the Registers of Civil Status;

(d) providing access to the Register of Civil Status; and

(e) the renewal of the worn out Registers of Civil Status.

(5) The Assistant Registrars and any other person authorized in writing by the Registrar shall perform the duties and have the powers conferred by this Act on the Registrar and delegated to them by the Registrar by instrument in writing.

(6) Where the position of Registrar is vacant, the Registrar of the High Court who is appointed under the Supreme Court Act, may perform the duties of the Registrar under this Act. *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 5. District Registrars

(1) The Registrar shall be assisted by a District Registrar for each district referred to in section 3.

(2) Subject to subsection (5), Cabinet may, upon the recommendation of the Minister, appoint a District Registrar for a maximum term of up to 6 years and such person is eligible for re-appointment. *(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(3) The Minister may by Regulations prescribe the qualifications of a District Registrar.

(4) A District Registrar is entitled to a stipend in an amount determined by Cabinet.

(5) A person is disqualified from being appointed as a District Registrar if he or she—

(a) is in part-time or full-time employment;

(b) has been convicted of a criminal offence except where the offence is a minor traffic offence or has been spent in accordance with the Criminal Records (Rehabilitation of Offenders) Act;

(c) is certified by 2 psychiatrists to be physically or mentally incapacitated by reason of unsoundness of mind; or

(d) is declared by a court to be a bankrupt.

(6) The Cabinet shall revoke the appointment of a person as a District Registrar where—

(a) he or she is convicted of a criminal offence, except where the offence is a minor traffic offence;

(b) he or she fails to comply with the provisions of this Act, the Civil Code, or any of the laws of Saint Lucia in relation to civil status;

(c) he or she is certified by 2 psychiatrists to be physically or mentally incapacitated by reason of unsoundness of mind; or

(d) he or she is declared by a court to be a bankrupt.

(7) If at any time before the revocation or expiry of the term of the appointment of a District Registrar, the District Registrar dies, the appointment of such District Registrar is deemed to have ceased from the time of his or her death.

### 6. Registers of Civil Status

(1) The Registers of Civil Status include the following:

(a) a register of births;

(b) a register of marriages;

(c) a register of deaths;

(d) a register of burials;

(e) a register of baptisms or dedications; and

(f) a register of adoptions.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(2) The Registers of Civil Status must be in the form prescribed and must be kept in triplicate as follows —

(a) two copies of each Register of Civil Status must be kept at the office of the Registrar as follows:

(i) one copy comprising all handwritten documents,

(ii) one electronic record; and

(b) one copy which may be electronic or otherwise kept in a place other than at the office of the Registrar.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(3) Where there is a variance between the handwritten copies of the Register of Civil Status and the electronic record, the copy comprising the handwritten documents prevails.

(4) A District Registrar shall sign the handwritten copies of the records of civil status except the records of adoption which the Registrar shall sign.

(5) The Minister responsible for civil status may, by Order published in the *Gazette*, set a date on which a depositary shall not be required to deposit records of baptisms or dedications or records of burials with the Registrar. *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 6A. REGISTER OF BIRTHS

The Register of Births shall have a designated area for an entry to be made with respect to —

(a) the Register —

(i) the district,

(ii) the year,

(iii) the page number,

(iv) the number of the register;

(b) the person, his or her —

(i) birth registration number,

(ii) date and place of birth,

(iii) the name,

(iv) the sex,

(v) where applicable, whether the person was stillborn;

(c) the parents of the person —

(i) the name, surname and dwelling place of the father,

(ii) the occupation of the father,

(iii) the name, surname and dwelling place of the mother,

(iv) the occupation of the mother,

(v) the age;

(d) the full name of the informant, his or her signature, occupation and dwelling place;

(e) registration —

(i) the date when the person was registered,

(ii) the signature of the Registrar,

(iii) the signature of the District Registrar,

(iv) the name, if added after registration of birth and date when added; and

(f) remarks.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

## PART 3 BIRTHS

### 7. Notification of births

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), for each child born in Saint Lucia, the responsible person shall give a notice in the prescribed form to the District Registrar within 5 days of the birth of the child.

(2) For the purpose of this section, “**responsible person**” means—

(a) if the child was born in a hospital or other health care facility or brought to the hospital or other health care facility within 24 hours after the birth, the person in charge of the hospital or other health care facility;

(b) if paragraph (a) does not apply—

(i) a doctor present at the birth, or

(ii) if a doctor was not present at the birth, a midwife present at the birth,

(iii) if neither a doctor or a midwife was present at the birth, a person other than the mother,

(iv) if the mother was alone at the birth, the mother unless she dies or abandons the child, in which case, the person who takes physical custody of the child or the person who discovers the child with the body of the mother.

(3) A person who fails to comply with this section commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding “$2,500.

### 8. Births to be registered in Saint Lucia

The birth of a child shall be registered in Saint Lucia if—

(a) the child was born in Saint Lucia;

(b) a judge of the Supreme Court finds that the child was born in Saint Lucia and makes an order that—

(i) the birth be registered, and

(ii) the prescribed particulars about the birth be registered.

### 9. Responsibility to apply to register a birth and form of application

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), if the birth of a child must be registered in Saint Lucia, the following persons shall apply in the prescribed form to the District Registrar to register the birth—

(a) the mother;

(b) either parent of the child if the parents are married; or

(c) if the child was found abandoned as a newborn or if the mother died before registration of the child, the person in whose care the child is.

(2) If the District Registrar does not receive an application under subsection (1), the District Registrar may require any one of the following persons to apply to register the birth—

(a) the person in charge of the place where the child was born;

(b) a person present at the birth; or

(c) a person who the District Registrar reasonably believes knows the relevant facts.

(3) A person shall, unless with reasonable excuse, comply with a request of the District Registrar under subsection (2).

(4) A person who fails to comply with this section commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.

### 10. Timeframe for the registration of the birth of a child

(1) A person shall, not later than 6 months after the birth of a child, apply to register the birth of that child. *(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1) the birth of any child may be registered after the expiration of 6 months—

(a) with the written authority of the Registrar;

(b) in accordance with any Regulations made for that purpose; and

(c) upon payment of the prescribed fees.

(3) The fact of the written authority having been given under subsection (2) must be entered in the Register of Births.

### 11. Registration of parental details

(1) This section applies if a person makes an application—

(a) to register the birth of a child; or

(b) to include information about the identity of a child's parent in the Register of Births after the child's birth has been registered.

(2) The District Registrar shall not include information that identifies a person as the parent of a child unless—

(a) the person signed a birth registration application and the District Registrar is satisfied that the person is a parent of the child; or

(b) the District Registrar is entitled under Book Seventh of the Civil Code to presume that the person is the parent of the child.

(3) The District Registrar may include information about the identity of a parent who did not sign the birth registration application if the Registrar is satisfied that the parent did not sign because—

(a) the parent is dead; or

(b) the parent is unable to sign the application.

(4) The District Registrar may include information about the identity of a parent who did not sign the birth registration application if the person who claims that someone else who is a parent of the child proves it by giving the District Registrar a copy of a court order to that effect or by such other means as is prescribed.

(5) Notwithstanding subsection (1), (2), (3) or (4), the father of a child of an unwed mother may by affidavit depose to the child's paternity, and if the affidavit is supported by the affidavit of the unwed mother deposing that the father is indeed the father of the child, the District Registrar shall upon receipt of the affidavits, no later than the 6 month period for registering a birth under section 10, enter the name of the father in the Register of Births.

## PART 4 NAME AND CHANGE OF NAME

### 12. Child's name

An application for registration of a birth, other than an application to register the birth of a stillborn child, must state the child's forename and surname.

### 13. Application to change child's forename within a year of birth

(1) This section applies if a child's birth was registered in Saint Lucia.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), the child's parents or guardians may apply, in the approved form, to change the child's forename in the Register of Births.

(3) One of the parents or guardians may apply to change the child's forename if —

(a) the parent is the only parent of the child entered in the Register of Births; or

(b) the other parent is dead; or

(c) the Registrar approves the change of the forename.

(4) An application under this section may be made only once and within a year of the child's birth.

(5) For the purpose of this section change of the forename includes adding a forename.

### 14. Issuance of updated records of a birth or adoption

(1) This section applies if —

(a) a person's birth was registered in Saint Lucia; or

(b) a person's adoption was registered under the Adoption Act.

(2) The Registrar may issue an updated record of a person's birth or adoption if—

(a) the Registrar decides that, because of the number of notes on the entry, it would be desirable to issue an updated record of births or adoptions; or

(b) the Registrar receives an application, in the approved form, to issue an updated record of the person's birth or adoption after a change of the person's name has been noted under this Act.

(3) An application to issue an updated record of a person's birth or adoption may only be made by—

(a) the person in the case of an adult; or

(b) a parent or guardian on the person's behalf in the case of a minor.

(4) The Registrar may issue an updated record of a person's birth or adoption by—

(a) duplicating the information other than information that has been superseded, in the entry for the person's birth or adoption in a new entry in the Register of Births, without indicating what information has been superseded;

(b) noting on the closed entry—

(i) that the record of birth or adoption has been updated, and

(ii) a reference to the new entry; and

(c) noting on the new entry—

(i) that the new entry was made under this section, and

(ii) a reference to the closed entry.

(5) The Registrar may issue a letter indicating the changes that have been made to the record from inception.

(6) For the purpose of this section “**closed entry**” means the entry for a person's birth or adoption prior to the issuance of an updated record of a person's birth or adoption.

### 15. Change of name by Registration OF NOTARIAL INSTRUMENT

From the date of commencement of this Act, a change of name by notarial instrument in Saint Lucia must not be given effect unless the notarial instrument is registered at the office of the Registrar and the Registry of Deeds and Mortgages. *(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

## PART 5 MARRIAGES

### 16. Types of marriages

(1) The following types of marriages may be lawfully solemnized in Saint Lucia in accordance with this Act—

(a) civil marriages; and

(b) religious marriages.

(2) Marriages may be performed—

(a) by licence granted by the Attorney General or after publication of banns, in the case of a religious marriage;

(b) by licence granted by the Attorney General and after publication of a notice, in the case of a civil marriage.

### 17. Performance of marriage

(1) A marriage may be performed—

(a) in the case of a civil marriage, by one of the following Civil Status Officers—

(i) the Registrar of Civil Status,

(ii) subject to subsection (2), a District Registrar, or

(iii) subject to subsections (5) and (6), a marriage officer appointed by Cabinet; or

(b) in the case of a religious marriage by—

(i) a Minister of Religion recognised under Article 94 of the Civil Code, or *(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(ii) subject to subsection (7), a Minister of Religion authorized by Cabinet on the recommendation of the Minister responsible for ecclesiastical affairs.

(2) With the prior permission of the Registrar, a District Registrar may perform a marriage ceremony in a district other than that to which he or she is appointed.

(3) The Minister may, by Order published in the Gazette, prescribe the fees for marriages performed by the Registrar or a District Registrar.

(4) The fees payable for the performance of a marriage by the Registrar or District Registrar is payable into and forms part of the Consolidated Fund.

(5) A person is disqualified from being appointed as a marriage officer if he or she is—

(a) in full-time employment;

(b) is under 45 years old;

(c) has been convicted of a criminal offence except where the offence is a minor offence or has been spent in accordance with the Criminal Records (Rehabilitation of Offenders) Act;

(d) is certified by 2 psychiatrists to be physically or mentally incapacitated by reason of unsoundness of mind; or

(e) is declared by a court to be a bankrupt.

(6) Cabinet shall revoke the appointment of a marriage officer who becomes disqualified under subsection (5).

(7) Cabinet may revoke the authorization of a Minister of Religion under subsection (1)(b) where a request for revocation is made by the religious organization which the Minister of Religion represents or where the Minister of Religion ceases to be affiliated to the denomination for which he or she is appointed.

(8) Cabinet may revoke the authorization of a District Registrar under subsection (1)(a) where a request for revocation is made by the Registrar. *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 18. Registration of marriages

(1) Civil Status Officers and Ministers of Religion are required to register marriages celebrated by them as a record of civil status in accordance with the Civil Code.

(2) A Civil Status Officer or Minister of Religion who fails to comply with this section commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000.

## PART 6 DEATH

### 19. Death in a house or elsewhere

(1) Where a person dies in a dwelling house it is the duty of —

(a) the nearest relatives of the deceased who are present at the time of death or in attendance during the last illness of the deceased;

(b) in default of the relatives referred to in paragraph (a), of every other relative of the deceased dwelling or being in the same District as the deceased; or

(c) in default of the relatives referred to in paragraphs (a) and (b), each person present at the death, and the occupier of the dwelling house in which, to his or her knowledge the death took place; and

(d) in default of the persons referred to in paragraphs (a), (b) and (c), the person causing the body of the deceased person to be buried,

to give to the best of his or her knowledge and belief to the District Registrar of the district in which the death took place, within 48 hours of the death, information of the particulars required to be registered concerning such death, and specified in the form prescribed.

(2) Where a person dies in a place which is not a dwelling house, or a dead body is found elsewhere than in a dwelling house, it is the duty of —

(a) every relative of the deceased person having knowledge of any of the particulars required to be registered concerning the death; or

(b) in default of such relative, of any person present at the death, and of every person finding or taking charge of the body,

to give to the District Registrar within 48 hours of the death such information in the prescribed form as is required to be registered concerning the death.

(3) Subject to section 22, a person who fails to comply with subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.

### 20. Requisition by Registrar of information concerning death

(1) Subject to section 21, where contrary to section 19, a death has not been registered or notice has not been given, the District Registrar may, at any time after the expiration of 48 hours and within 3 months from the day of the death or the finding of the dead body, by notice in writing require any person—

(a) to give information concerning the death;

(b) to attend personally at the District Registrar's office, or at any other place appointed by the District Registrar within his or her district, within such time, not less than 7 days after the receipt of the notice nor more than 6 months after the death or finding of the dead body as may be specified in the notice; and

(c) to give the information to the best of the person's knowledge and belief,

and it is the duty of the person, unless the death is registered before the expiration of the time specified in the notice, to comply with the requirement.

(2) Subject to section 22, a person who fails to comply with subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.

### 21. Registration of death on receipt of information under section 19 or 20 and medical certificate

Where at any time within six months after the date of a death or of the finding of a dead body, the District Registrar receives—

(a) the information required for registration of a death from a person as required under sections 19 or 20; and

(b) a medical certificate of the cause of death,

the District Registrar shall record the death in the copy of the Register of Deaths held by the District Registrar.

### 22. Information from coroner

(1) Where an inquest is held on any dead body, the coroner shall inquire into the particulars required to be registered concerning the death and shall send to the District Registrar, within 3 days after his or her finding on the inquest, a certificate under his or her hand giving information concerning the death and specifying his or her finding with respect to the particulars and to the cause of death, and specifying the time and place at which the inquest was held.

(2) Where the District Registrar receives a certificate from the coroner, the District Registrar shall—

(a) if it has not been previously registered in the Register of Deaths, record the death together with the date of the inquisition, the name of the coroner and his or her finding; or

(b) if the death has been registered, enter the date of the inquisition, the name of the coroner and his or her finding; and

(c) forward the record of the death to the Registrar.

(3) Where an inquest is held on any dead body, a person shall not with respect to such dead body or death, be liable to give information under section 19 or to attend on a requisition of a District Registrar under section 20 or be subject to any penalty for failing to give any information under section 19 or 20.

### 23. Time restrictions for registration

(1) After the expiration of 6 months after any death or after the finding of any dead body elsewhere than in a house, that death must not be registered except—

(a) with the written authority of the Registrar;

(b) in accordance with the Regulations;

(c) upon payment of the prescribed fee.

(2) The fact of the authority having been given under paragraph (a) of subsection (1) must be entered in the copy of the Register of Deaths held by the District Registrar.

### 24. Requirement for certificate of death

(1) A person shall not bury a dead body, whether or not the dead body is of a still born baby, unless the person is in possession of a certificate of death issued by the District Registrar.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.

## PART 7 RECTIFICATION OF RECORDS OF CIVIL STATUS

## (Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)

### 25. Rectification by REGISTRAR

(1) The Registrar may, at his or her instance or at the instance of an interested party, rectify incorrect or incomplete records of civil status.

(2) The Registrar may delegate his or her functions pursuant to this section to one or more Assistant Registrars or Adjudicators appointed by the Judicial and Legal Service Commission.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 25A. RECTIFICATION OF A CLERICAL ERROR IN A RECORD OF CIVIL STATUS

(1) The Registrar may, at the instance of an applicant or at his or her instance, rectify a clerical error in a record of civil status.

(2) In this section, **“clerical error”** means an error made in an entry in a record of civil status with respect to —

(a) the sex of the person;

(b) the year of the record;

(c) the date of birth of the person;

(d) the number of the register;

(e) the page number;

(f) the date of registration; and

(g) any other clerical error, as determined by the Registrar.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 25B. Insertion of a person's name on a Birth Certificate

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), where there is no entry or a partial entry made with respect to the name of a person in his or her Birth Certificate, the Registrar may, at the instance of the person who seeks to have his or her name inserted on the birth certificate, insert the name of that person in his or her Birth Certificate as recorded and entered in his or her record of baptism or dedication.

(2) Where the Registrar inserts the name of a person in his or her Birth Certificate under subsection (1), he or she shall record and enter the name of that person in the Register of Civil Status and any other record books kept by the Registrar to reflect the same.

(3) A person shall submit the following —

(a) his or her record of baptism or dedication;

(b) the prescribed fee; and

(c) any other document as requested by the Registrar.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 25C. Change of an entry in a record of civil status

(1) Subject to subsection (4), a person who wishes to change an entry to his or her record of civil status with respect to his or her forename or surname shall provide satisfactory evidence to the Registrar of consistently using, from the time of his or her birth, the forename or surname that he or she wishes to have registered.

(2) In support of an application to change an entry in a record of civil status in accordance with subsection (1), a person shall submit the following —

(a) his or her Birth Certificate;

(b) his or her record of baptism or dedication;

(c) the prescribed fee; and

(d) any other documents requested by the Registrar.

(3) A change in an entry in a record made by the Registrar under subsection (1) to a record of civil status may be made only once.

(4) A person who wishes to change his or her name, after a rectification by the Registrar under subsection (1), must do so by a Deed Poll.

(5) For the purposes of this section **“satisfactory evidence”** includes —

(a) an affidavit from the person who wishes to correct his or her record of civil status;

(b) an affidavit from two persons who have known the person for a period of time as prescribed by the Registrar; and

(c) where the person is an adult, a valid national identification document in the form of —

(i) a national identification card,

(ii) a passport, or

(iii) a driver's licence.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 25D. Rectification process

(1) A person who wishes to rectify his or her record of civil status shall apply to the Registrar in the prescribed form.

(2) A parent or guardian may apply, in the prescribed form, to rectify a record of civil status of a person who is their issue.

(3) (a)   Subject to paragraph (b), a person who wishes to rectify a record of civil status of another person, shall apply to the Registrar in the prescribed form, supported by an authorisation letter in the prescribed form.

(b) Where a person is unable to produce an authorization letter as referred to in paragraph (a), the Registrar may accept any other document or information as required.

(4) Where information is not readily available to support an application to rectify a record of civil status, the Registrar may accept any other document or information as required.

(5) The Registrar may refer an application to rectify an error in an entry in a record of civil status to a Judge of the High Court for determination.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 25E. Rectification Order

The Registrar shall issue an Order to rectify a record of civil status which shall be recorded and entered in the Register of Civil Status and any other record books kept by the Registrar to reflect the same.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

## PART 8 MISCELLANEOUS

### 26. Annual abstract of births and deaths

The Registrar shall in every year furnish to the Minister a general abstract of the number of births and deaths registered in the preceding year in such form as the Minister may prescribe.

### 27. Quarterly transmission of copies of registers

(1) A District Registrar shall transmit to the Registrar four times in every year, on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October, respectively, a true copy, certified by him or her under his or her hand, of all the entries of births and deaths in the Register kept by him or her and made since the last quarterly return.

(2) The Registrar shall keep and preserve the copy of the entries of births and deaths in the Registers as a record in his or her office, and shall make general indexes of the same in books to be furnished to him or her for such purpose at the public expense.

### 28. Offences

(1) A person shall not—

(a) wilfully give any false answer to any question put to him or her by a District Registrar relating to the particulars required to be registered concerning any birth or death, or wilfully give to a District Registrar any false information concerning any birth or death, or the cause of death;

(b) wilfully make any false certificate or declaration under or for the purposes of this Act, or forge or falsify any such certificate or declaration or any order under this Act, or knowing any such certificate, declaration or order to be false or forged use the same as true or give or send the same as true to any person;

(c) wilfully make, give or use any false statement or representation that a child is born alive having been still-born, or as to the body of a deceased person or still-born child in any coffin, or falsely pretend that any child born alive was still-born;

(d) make any false statement with intent to have the statement entered in any Register of Births or Deaths; *(Amended by Act 10 of 2014)*

(e) wilfully provide false information or fraudulent documents to the Registrar in an application to rectify his or her record of civil status; *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(f) wilfully provide false information or fraudulent documents to the Registrar in an application to register a child or to include information of the parental details of a child. *(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years.

### 29. General penalty

A person who commits an offence under this Act for which no penalty is specified is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.

### 30. Regulations

The Minister may make Regulations —

(a) for the management, conduct and control of the offices of the Registrar and of the District Registrars and for the discharge of the duties of the Registrar and of a District Registrar;

(b) for any prescribed forms, matter or thing; and

(c) generally for carrying out the provisions of this Act.

*(Substituted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 30A. Amendment of Schedule

The Minister may, by Order published in the *Gazette*, amend the Schedule to this Act.

*(Inserted by Act 10 of 2014)*

### 31. Consequential amendment to Civil Code

The Civil Code is amended as provided for in the Schedule.

### 32. Repeal

The Civil Status Act is repealed.

### 33. Transitional

A District Registrar appointed under the repealed Act shall continue to hold office and to be paid in accordance with the repealed Act until such time as his or her appointment is terminated by Cabinet.
